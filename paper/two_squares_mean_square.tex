\begin{abstract}
Let $m,m'$ be coprime odd integers, $\Theta_{m,m'}(z)=\theta^2(mz)\theta^2(m'z)$, $G_{m,m'}$ its cuspidal part and $L=4mm'$, so that $a_{G}(N)=T(m,m';N)-M(m,m';N)$ is the error term in the number of representations of $N$ by $m(x^2+y^2)+m'(z^2+w^2)$. Part~I proved $\|G_{m,m'}\|^2\ll_\e(mm')^{\e}$ for the Petersson norm and deduced the level of distribution $1/2$ for the weighted sequence $r(n)r(N-n)$ and the bounds $\Omega(n(N-n))\le8$, $\max(\Omega(n),\Omega(N-n))\le5$ for every large $N\equiv2\pmod4$. Here we prove a mean-square bound $\sum_{N\le Y}|a_G(N)|^2\le4\pi e^2Y(1+54Y/L)\|G\|^2$ for every $G\in S_2(\Gamma_0(L))$ by a covering argument; for $G_{m,m'}$ it says that the root mean square of $(T-M)/\sqrt N$ is $\ll(mm')^{-1/2+\e}$, and it gives the level of distribution $1-\e$ for the weighted sequence for all but $O(X^{1-\delta})$ integers $N\le X$, and for those $N$ the bounds $5$ and $4$ (a result which, as an arithmetic statement about almost all $N$, is superseded by the circle method, which gives $N=p+q$ with $p\equiv q\equiv1\pmod 4$). We reformulate the uniform bound of Part~I in terms of Poincar\'e series, state the individual bound $|T-M|\ll N^{1/2+\e}(mm')^{-1/2+\e}$ suggested by the data, and show that any power saving in $mm'$ for individual $N$ raises the level of distribution for every $N$ beyond $1/2$. Finally we prove that the norm depends only on $mm'$ (an Atkin--Lehner involution maps $G_{m,m'}$ to $G_{1,mm'}$), the lower bound $\|G_{m,m'}\|^2\ge\frac1\pi(\log mm')^2-O\bigl(\log(2mm')(\log\log3mm')^{C}\bigr)$, by integrating $|G|^2$ over the parts of the fundamental domain above the Atkin--Lehner cusps at which $G_{m,m'}$ is transformed into $G_{1,mm'}$, whose first $mm'$ coefficients are $r(n)-M(1,mm';n)$, and the matching upper bound $\|G_{m,m'}\|^2\ll(\log3mm')^2(\log\log3mm')^{C}$, so that the norm is of the exact order $(\log mm')^2$ when $mm'$ has boundedly many prime factors and the uniform bound for $T-M$ holds with $(\log3mm')(\log\log3mm')^C$ in place of $(mm')^\e$. Evaluating the Parseval sums over the cusps at both heights, $1$ and $\sqrt3/2$, gives the asymptotic formula $\|G_{m,m'}\|^2=\frac1\pi\sum_{\delta\mid mm',\ \delta^2<mm'}\delta^{-1}(\log(mm'/\delta^2))^2+O(\log(2mm')(\log\log3mm')^C)$ for square-free $mm'$, in particular $\|G_{1,p}\|^2=\frac1\pi(\log p)^2+O(\log p)$. By an explicit Rankin--Selberg unfolding, $\langle\Theta_{m,m'},f(d\,\cdot)\rangle=\pm\frac4{\pi^2}\prod_{p\mid mm'/M_1}(1+\chi_{-4}(p))\,d^{-1}L(1,f)L(1,f\otimes\chi_{-4})$ for every newform $f$ of level $2^eM_1\mid4mm'$ and every $d$, with no other local factors, so these bounds are bounds for the second moment of $L(\frac12,f)L(\frac12,f\otimes\chi_{-4})$ over the family, and the asymptotic formula is an asymptotic formula for this second moment. Since every newform of level $4mm'$ has the same local component at $2$, the Atkin--Lehner symmetries of $\Theta_{1,mm'}$ imply that no newform of level $4mm'$ occurs in $G_{m,m'}$ when $\chi_{-4}(mm')=-1$, and then $L(1,\varphi)L(1,\varphi\otimes\chi_{-4})=0$ for all these newforms. Numerically, the regions above all the cusps account for $93$--$98\%$ of the norm; there is an exact check at the level $20$, and the supremum of $|T-M|/\sqrt N$ over $N$ exceeds the root mean square by a bounded factor at every level computed. Finally, replacing the vector sieve of Part~I by the two-dimensional sieve of Diamond, Halberstam and Richert, whose functions $F_2,f_2$ we compute, gives $\Omega(n(N-n))\le7$ with $\max(\Omega(n),\Omega(N-n))\le5$ for every large $N\equiv2\pmod4$ from the same level $1/2$; the two inequalities for $F_2,f_2$ that this needs are certified in interval arithmetic.
\end{abstract}

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

We keep the notation of \cite{ChenI}, referred to as Part~I. Thus $r(n)$ is the number of representations of $n$ as a sum of two squares, $m,m'$ are coprime odd positive integers,
\begin{gather*}
T(m,m';N)=\sum_{ma+m'b=N}r(a)r(b),\qquad L=4mm',\\
\Theta_{m,m'}(z)=\theta^2(mz)\theta^2(m'z)=\sum_{N\ge0}T(m,m';N)e(Nz)\in M_2(\Gamma_0(L)),
\end{gather*}
$\Theta_{m,m'}=E+G$ is the decomposition into the Eisenstein part, whose coefficients are the Siegel main terms $M(m,m';N)$, and the cuspidal part $G=G_{m,m'}\in S_2(\Gamma_0(L))$, so that
\[
a_G(N)=T(m,m';N)-M(m,m';N)\qquad\text{for every }N\ge1 .
\]
The Petersson norm is $\|G\|^2=\int_{\Gamma_0(L)\backslash\HH}|G|^2\,\dd x\,\dd y$. Lemma~2.1 of Part~I, which is Waibel's theorem \cite[Theorem~1]{Waibel} for the form $Q_{m,m'}=m(x^2+y^2)+m'(z^2+w^2)$, gives $\|G\|\ll(mm')^{1/4+\e}$, and the first two versions of Part~I deduced from it, through a Hecke operator, the uniform bound $a_G(N)\ll d(N)N^{1/2}(mm')^{1/4+\e}$ for $(N,mm')=1$, $v_2(N)\le1$, the level of distribution $2/5$ and the bounds $9$ and $6$, and conjectured (Conjecture~5.1 there) that the factor $(mm')^{1/4}$ can be removed. Version~3 of Part~I, which this paper accompanies, proves that the norm itself is much smaller than Waibel's general bound, by computing the expansion of $\Theta_{m,m'}$ at every cusp of $\Gamma_0(L)$ with Waibel's method, and draws the consequences. We recall what we use.

\begin{theorem}[Part~I, Theorems~1.2 and 2.3]\label{thm:norm}
Let $m,m'$ be coprime odd positive integers.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em]
\item $\|G_{m,m'}\|^2\ll_\e(mm')^{\e}$.
\item For $N\ge1$ with $(N,mm')=1$ and $v_2(N)\le1$,
\[
T(m,m';N)=M(m,m';N)+O_\e\bigl(d(N)\,N^{1/2}(mm')^{\e}\bigr);
\]
for cube-free $m,m'$ and every $N$ with $v_2(N)\le1$ the error term is $O_\e(d(N)^4N^{1/2}(mm')^\e)$ (Part~I, Corollary~2.7).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Part~I deduces from (ii) that $r(n)r(N-n)$ has level of distribution $1/2$ (Theorem~3.2 there) and, with sieve parameters certified in interval arithmetic, that for every sufficiently large $N\equiv2\pmod4$
\begin{multline*}
\#\bigl\{1\le n<N:\ n=x^2+y^2,\ N-n=z^2+w^2,\ \Omega(n(N-n))\le 8,\ \max(\Omega(n),\Omega(N-n))\le 5\bigr\}\\
\ge\ 0.23\,\Sg_r(N)\,\frac{N}{(\log N)^2},\qquad \Sg_r(N)=\prod_{\substack{p\mid N\\ p>2}}\frac{p-1}{p-2},
\end{multline*}
(Theorem~1.1 there; versions 1--2 had $9$ and $6$). The proof of (i) shows that the expansion of $\Theta_{m,m'}$ at every cusp is again a theta series of a form $A(x_1^2+x_2^2)+B(x_3^2+x_4^2)$ with $AB\asymp mm'$, whose coefficients are $O(n^\e)$ for $n\ll mm'$ because $A\alpha+B\beta=n$ has $O(1)$ solutions, and suggests $\|G_{m,m'}\|^2\asymp(\log mm')^2$ (Part~I, Remark~2.13); numerically, $\|G_{m,m'}\|^2\approx0.37\,(\log mm')^2$ (Section~\ref{sec:num}). Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} below proves the lower bound, and Section~\ref{sec:lower} decomposes the norm over the cusps.

The first theme of this paper is the average over $N$ of the error term, which is of independent interest and gives a stronger level of distribution for almost all $N$.

\begin{theorem}[Mean square]\label{thm:A}
Let $L\ge1$, $G\in S_2(\Gamma_0(L))$ and $Y\ge1$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A}
\sum_{n\le Y}|a_G(n)|^2\ \le\ 4\pi e^2\,Y\Bigl(1+\frac{54\,Y}{L}\Bigr)\|G\|^2 .
\end{equation}
In particular, for coprime odd $m,m'$, $G=G_{m,m'}$ and $Y\ge mm'$, by Theorem~\ref{thm:norm}(i),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A2}
\sum_{N\le Y}\bigl|T(m,m';N)-M(m,m';N)\bigr|^2\ \ll_\e\ Y^2\,(mm')^{-1+\e},
\end{equation}
and hence, since $\sum_{Y/2<N\le Y}|a_G(N)|^2/N\le(2/Y)\sum_{N\le Y}|a_G(N)|^2$, the root mean square of $(T-M)/\sqrt N$ over $Y/2<N\le Y$ is $\ll(mm')^{-1/2+\e}$.
\end{theorem}

The proof of \eqref{eq:A} is a classical covering argument and does not use the Petersson formula; the dependence on the level is exactly the factor $Y/L$, i.e.\ the volume of $\Gamma_0(L)\backslash\HH$. The bound \eqref{eq:A2} is the exact order of magnitude: by the Rankin--Selberg method the mean square of $(T-M)/\sqrt N$ is asymptotic to $16\pi^2\|G\|^2/\vol(\Gamma_0(L)\backslash\HH)$, and Table~\ref{tab:ms} shows the data.

Averaging over $N$ is natural for the arithmetic application, since the sieve of Part~I is applied to one $N$ at a time and an exceptional set of $N$ can be tolerated. Let, as in Part~I, $N\equiv2\pmod4$, $\cA=\{n\le N:\ (n,N)=1\}$, $w(n)=r(n)r(N-n)$, and for coprime square-free $d,e$ with $(de,2N)=1$ let $R(d,e)=R(d,e;N)$ be the remainder in
\[
\sum_{\substack{n\in\cA\\ d\mid n,\ e\mid N-n}}w(n)=X_N\,h(d)h(e)+R(d,e;N)
\]
(this is $S(d,e)=M(d,e)+R(d,e)$ of Part~I, Section~3; we write $X_N$ for the main term $X$ there, to avoid a clash with the length $X$ of the range of $N$ below).

\begin{theorem}[Level of distribution $1-\e$ for almost all $N$]\label{thm:B}
Let $0<\e<1/20$, $X\ge X_0(\e)$ and $D=X^{1-3\e}$. Then
\[
\sum_{\substack{X/2<N\le X\\ N\equiv2\ (4)}}\ \sum_{\substack{de\le D,\ (d,e)=1\\ (de,2N)=1}}\mu^2(de)\,d_3(de)\,|R(d,e;N)|\ \ll_\e\ X^{2-\e/2}.
\]
Consequently, for all but $O_\e(X^{1-\e/4})$ integers $N\equiv2\pmod4$ in $(X/2,X]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B}
\sum_{\substack{de\le N^{1-3\e}\\ (d,e)=1,\ (de,2N)=1}}\mu^2(de)\,d_3(de)\,|R(d,e;N)|\ \le\ N^{1-\e/5}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The sieve of Part~I, Section~4, uses the level of distribution only through \eqref{eq:B}; running it at the level $\theta=19/20$ gives the following.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:C}
For all but $O(X^{1-\delta})$ integers $N\equiv2\pmod 4$ in $(X/2,X]$, with an absolute $\delta>0$,
\begin{multline*}
\#\bigl\{1\le n<N:\ n=x^2+y^2,\ N-n=z^2+w^2,\ \Omega(n(N-n))\le 5,\ \max(\Omega(n),\Omega(N-n))\le 4\bigr\}\\
\ge\ 0.34\,\Sg_r(N)\,\frac{N}{(\log N)^2},
\end{multline*}
the integers counted being coprime to $N$ and products of distinct primes $\equiv1\pmod4$ exceeding $N^{2/25}$.
\end{corollary}

The certified sieve parameters are $\alpha=2/25$, $\lambda=1/3$, $\beta_1=69/100$ (Section~\ref{sec:B}). The pairs $(5,4)$ for almost all $N$ and $(8,5)$ for every $N$ should be compared with $(9,6)$ for every $N$, which is what Waibel's bound alone gives (Part~I, Remark~4.5). A floating-point scan (Figure~\ref{fig:thr}) shows that the best attainable value of $1/\lambda+1/\beta_1$ drops below $4$ only for $\theta>0.88$; at $\theta=19/20$ the scan gives $3.83$ (at $\alpha=0.10$, $\beta_1=0.47$, $\lambda=0.59$, with $1/\lambda+2/\beta_1=5.96$), i.e.\ the pair $(5,3)$, which we have not certified, for the reason explained next.

Corollary~\ref{cor:C} must also be compared with what the circle method gives for almost all $N$. By the method of Montgomery and Vaughan \cite{MV}, carried out with the characters modulo $4$ (see \cite{BauerWang} for Goldbach's problem with primes in prescribed residue classes), all but $O(X^{1-\delta})$ integers $N\equiv2\pmod4$ up to $X$ are sums $p+q$ of two primes $p\equiv q\equiv1\pmod 4$, with $\gg\Sg_r(N)N/(\log N)^2$ representations; such primes are sums of two squares, so for almost all $N$ the pair $(1,1)$ holds with the count of Corollary~\ref{cor:C}. As an arithmetic statement about almost all $N$, Corollary~\ref{cor:C} is therefore far from new. What is new is Theorem~\ref{thm:B}, a level of distribution $1-\e$ for the weighted sequence $r(n)r(N-n)$, together with the observation that the sieve of Part~I extracts $(5,4)$ from it with no further input; the sieve route adds something only for \emph{every} $N$, where the circle method gives nothing (Goldbach's problem) and where Part~I gives $(8,5)$. Section~\ref{sec:spectral} formulates the individual bound that would raise the level to $1-\e$ for every $N$ and with it give $(5,4)$ for every $N$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:beyond}).

The third theme is the size of the norm itself. Theorem~\ref{thm:norm}(i) is an upper bound. The proof in Part~I shows that the expansion of $\Theta_{m,m'}$ at every cusp of $\Gamma_0(L)$ is a theta series of a form $A(x_1^2+x_2^2)+B(x_3^2+x_4^2)$ with $AB\asymp mm'$; at the four cusps which are the images of $\infty$ under the Atkin--Lehner involutions $W_m$, $W_{4m}$, $W_{m'}$, $W_{4m'}$ this form is $x_1^2+x_2^2+mm'(x_3^2+x_4^2)$, whose first $mm'$ representation numbers are the $r(n)$ themselves, and there $G_{m,m'}$ is transformed, exactly, into $G_{1,mm'}$. Integrating $|G|^2$ over the parts of the fundamental domain above these four cusps gives a lower bound.

\begin{theorem}[Lower bound for the norm]\label{thm:lower}
Let $m,m'$ be coprime odd positive integers with $mm'>1$, and let $a(n)=a_{G_{1,mm'}}(n)=T(1,mm';n)-M(1,mm';n)$, so that $a(n)=r(n)-M(1,mm';n)$ for $1\le n<mm'$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em]
\item $\displaystyle \|G_{m,m'}\|^2\ \ge\ \frac1{4\pi}\sum_{Q\in\{m,\,4m,\,m',\,4m'\}}\ \sum_{n\ge1}\frac{|a(n)|^2}{n}\,e^{-4\pi n/Q}.$
\item $\|G_{m,m'}\|=\|G_{1,mm'}\|$: the norm depends only on the product $mm'$.
\item There is an absolute constant $C$ such that
\[
\|G_{m,m'}\|^2\ \ge\ \frac1\pi(\log mm')^2-C\log(2mm')\,(\log\log 3mm')^{C}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Part (ii) is the Atkin--Lehner involution $W_m$, which maps $\Theta_{m,m'}$ to $\chi_{-4}(m)\Theta_{1,mm'}$ and is an isometry; (iii) is (i) for the pair $(1,mm')$ at its two cusps of widths $mm'$ and $4mm'$. Together with Theorem~\ref{thm:norm}(i), $(\log mm')^2\ll\|G_{m,m'}\|^2\ll_\e(mm')^\e$ for $mm'\ge C'$, and the exponent $\e$ cannot be replaced by $0$. In fact the factor $(mm')^\e$ can be removed altogether. Theorem~\ref{thm:upper} in Section~\ref{subsec:upper} replaces the two $\e$-losses in the proof of Part~I by mean values of $r(\alpha)^2$ and of the local densities and gives
\begin{gather*}
\|G_{m,m'}\|^2\ll(\log3mm')^2(\log\log3mm')^{C},\\
\text{hence}\qquad \|G_{m,m'}\|^2\asymp_k(\log mm')^2\qquad\text{whenever }\omega(mm')\le k,\\
\text{and Theorem~\ref{thm:norm}(ii) holds with }(\log3mm')(\log\log3mm')^{C}\text{ in place of }(mm')^\e .
\end{gather*}
The two bounds come from the same Parseval sums over the cusps, taken above height $1$ (where the regions are disjoint) and above height $\sqrt3/2$ (where they cover the fundamental domain); evaluating the sums at both heights gives the norm asymptotically, for square-free $mm'$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:asymp} in Section~\ref{subsec:asymp}):

\begin{theorem}[The asymptotic formula]\label{thm:asympintro}
Let $m,m'$ be coprime odd square-free positive integers and $n=mm'$. Then
\[
\|G_{m,m'}\|^2=\frac1\pi\sum_{\substack{\delta\mid n\\ \delta^2<n}}\frac1\delta\Bigl(\log\frac n{\delta^2}\Bigr)^2+O\bigl(\log(2n)(\log\log3n)^{C}\bigr);
\]
the terms with $\delta>1$ lie between $0$ and $(\log n)^2(\sigma(n)/n-1)$, so that $\|G_{m,m'}\|^2\sim\frac1\pi(\log mm')^2$ when $\sum_{p\mid mm'}1/p\to0$, and $\|G_{1,p}\|^2=\frac1\pi(\log p)^2+O(\log p)$ for primes $p$.
\end{theorem}

On the spectral side, Theorem~\ref{thm:RS} makes the Rankin--Selberg structure of Part~I, Section~5.5, explicit: for every newform $\varphi$ of weight $2$ and level $4mm'$ ($mm'$ square-free),
\[
\langle\Theta_{m,m'},\varphi\rangle=\frac4{\pi^2}\,\chi_{-4}(m')\,\eta_{m'}(\varphi)\,L(1,\varphi)\,L(1,\varphi\otimes\chi_{-4}),
\]
with no further local factors; so the norm bounds are statements about the second moment of $L(\frac12,\varphi)L(\frac12,\varphi\otimes\chi_{-4})$ over the family of level $4mm'$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:newmoment}), and Theorem~\ref{thm:asympintro} gives this second moment asymptotically: for $p$ prime, $\sum_f\gamma_fL(1,f)^2L(1,f\otimes\chi_{-4})^2=\frac{\pi^3}{16}(\log p)^2+O(\log p)$ over the newforms $f$ of levels $p$, $2p$, $4p$, with the explicit weights $\gamma_f$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:newmoment} (Corollary~\ref{cor:asympL}). The inequality (i) is nearly an equality when $m=1$: the four regions then cover the fraction $5/(2\pi)=0.80$ of the area of $\Gamma_0(L)\backslash\HH$, and numerically they carry $98\%$ of the norm (Table~\ref{tab:cusps}). The proof is in Section~\ref{sec:lower}, which also computes, for square-free $m,m'$, the contribution of every cusp of the fundamental domain of the pair $(m,m')$: these cusp sums depend on the factorisation (for $m\approx m'$ they are $\frac1\pi((\log m)^2+(\log m')^2)+\frac5{\pi^2}\log m\log m'+\cdots$, a sum over the divisors of $mm'$) although their total, the norm, does not; the empirical law $0.37(\log mm')^2$ of Section~\ref{sec:num} is the leading term $\frac1\pi(\log mm')^2$ of (iii) together with the positive lower-order terms, which are still large at $mm'\le10^6$.

The fourth theme is the sieve itself. Part~I sifts $n$ and $N-n$ by the vector sieve of Br\"udern and Fouvry, two linear sieves combined; but $n(N-n)$ is a sieve problem of dimension $2$ (Remark~\ref{rem:dim2}), and the two-dimensional sieve of Diamond, Halberstam and Richert \cite{DHR,DH}, with Richert's weights, has the smaller sieving limit $\beta_2=4.2665$. Section~\ref{sec:dim2} computes its sieve functions $F_2,f_2$ (we determine the constants $\alpha_2=5.3577$, $\beta_2=4.2665$ of \cite{DHR} independently) and obtains, from the same level of distribution $1/2$ of Part~I,
\[
\Omega(n(N-n))\le7,\qquad\max(\Omega(n),\Omega(N-n))\le5
\]
for $\ge0.25\,\Sg_r(N)N/(\log N)^2$ integers $n$, for every large $N\equiv2\pmod 4$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:dim2}): $(7,5)$ in place of $(8,5)$. The sieve functions enter through two numerical inequalities, $f_2(9)\ge0.9995$ and a bound for an integral of $F_2$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:cert}), which we certify in interval arithmetic from the constants $\alpha_2,\beta_2$ computed in ball arithmetic by Booker and Browning \cite{BB}, as Part~I does for the linear sieve functions; the theorem is unconditional.

Section~\ref{sec:spectral} restates the uniform bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:norm}(ii) in terms of Poincar\'e series and formulates the individual bound, Section~\ref{sec:num} contains the numerical evidence: the data of Part~I, an exact check of the whole numerical chain at the level $20$, the empirical law $\|G_{m,m'}\|^2\approx0.37(\log mm')^2$, which corrects a remark in the first two versions of Part~I, and the supremum of $|T-M|/\sqrt N$ over $N$; Section~\ref{sec:lower} proves Theorems~\ref{thm:lower} and \ref{thm:asympintro}, computes the Rankin--Selberg coefficients and the spectral support, and decomposes the norm over the cusps; Section~\ref{sec:dim2} contains the two-dimensional sieve, shows that the remainder it uses at the modulus $d$ is the coefficient of the projection of the cusp form onto the joint $(+1)$-eigenspace of the Atkin--Lehner involutions $W_p$, $p\mid d$ (so that all oldforms of odd level cancel in it), and explains what the weights $r(n)r(N-n)$ cost, namely the sieve dimension; and Section~\ref{sec:beyond} describes the sum over the moduli that a level beyond $1/2$ for every $N$ would require, writes its leading term as a trilinear form in classical Kloosterman sums $S(16N,m|h|^2;m'k)$ with the modulus in the progression $c\equiv0\pmod{m'}$, and gives numerical evidence that its terms cancel like random signs.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}}\label{sec:A}

Let $G(z)=\sum_{n\ge1}a(n)e(nz)\in S_2(\Gamma_0(L))$ and put $F(z)=y^2|G(z)|^2$, $z=x+iy$. Since $G(\gamma z)=(cz+d)^2G(z)$ and $\operatorname{Im}(\gamma z)=y/|cz+d|^2$ for $\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\ c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_0(L)$, the function $F$ is $\Gamma_0(L)$-invariant, and with the invariant measure $\dd\mu=y^{-2}\dd x\,\dd y$ we have $\|G\|^2=\int_{\cF}F\,\dd\mu$ for any fundamental domain $\cF$ of $\Gamma_0(L)$.

Let $y_0=1/(4\pi Y)$ and $R=\{x+iy:\ 0\le x<1,\ y_0\le y\le2y_0\}$ (half-open in $x$, so that each orbit of the translations $z\mapsto z+b$ meets $R$ at most once). By Parseval, $\int_0^1|G(x+iy)|^2\,\dd x=\sum_n|a(n)|^2e^{-4\pi ny}$, hence
\[
\int_R|G|^2\,\dd x\,\dd y=\sum_n|a(n)|^2\int_{y_0}^{2y_0}e^{-4\pi ny}\,\dd y\ \ge\ \frac{e^{-2}}{4\pi Y}\sum_{n\le Y}|a(n)|^2 ,
\]
because $4\pi ny\le 8\pi Yy_0=2$ for $n\le Y$, $y\le2y_0$. On the other hand $\int_R|G|^2\,\dd x\,\dd y=\int_RF\,\dd\mu$, and
\[
\int_RF\,\dd\mu=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_0(L)/\{\pm1\}}\int_{R\cap\gamma\cF}F\,\dd\mu=\int_{\cF}F(w)\,\#\{\gamma:\ \gamma w\in R\}\,\dd\mu(w)\ \le\ \mu_R\,\|G\|^2,
\]
where $\mu_R=\sup_{z\in R}\#\{\gamma\in\Gamma_0(L)/\{\pm1\}:\ \gamma z\in R\}$ (if $\#\{\gamma:\gamma w\in R\}\ne0$ for some $w\in\cF$, pick $\gamma_0$ with $z=\gamma_0w\in R$; then $\gamma\mapsto\gamma\gamma_0^{-1}$ is a bijection from $\{\gamma:\gamma w\in R\}$ onto $\{\gamma:\gamma z\in R\}$, so the count at $w$ is at most $\mu_R$). It remains to bound $\mu_R$. Fix $z=x+iy\in R$ and let $\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\ c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_0(L)$ with $\gamma z\in R$.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item If $c=0$, then $\gamma=\pm\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&b\\ 0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and $\gamma z=z+b$, so $x+b\in[0,1)$ forces $b=0$: one element.
\item If $c\ne0$, we may take $c>0$, since $\gamma$ and $-\gamma$ are the same element of $\Gamma_0(L)/\{\pm1\}$. Then $L\mid c$, and $\operatorname{Im}(\gamma z)=y/|cz+d|^2\in[y_0,2y_0]$ together with $y\in[y_0,2y_0]$ gives $|cz+d|^2\in[1/2,2]$. Since $|cz+d|^2=(cx+d)^2+c^2y^2$, this forces $c^2y^2\le2$, hence $c\le\sqrt2/y_0=4\sqrt2\,\pi Y$, and $|cx+d|\le\sqrt2$. The number of positive multiples $c$ of $L$ with $c\le4\sqrt2\pi Y$ is $\lfloor4\sqrt2\pi Y/L\rfloor\le4\sqrt2\pi\,Y/L$; for each $c$ there are at most $3$ integers $d$ with $|cx+d|\le\sqrt2$; and for each pair $(c,d)$ the elements of $\Gamma_0(L)$ with bottom row $(c,d)$ are $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&k\\ 0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\gamma_0$, $k\in\Z$, whose images $\gamma_0z+k$ have real part in $[0,1)$ for exactly one $k$. This gives at most $4\sqrt2\pi\cdot3\,Y/L=12\sqrt2\pi\,Y/L<54\,Y/L$ elements.
\end{itemize}
Hence $\mu_R\le1+54Y/L$, and
\[
\sum_{n\le Y}|a(n)|^2\le 4\pi e^2Y\int_RF\,\dd\mu\le 4\pi e^2\,Y\Bigl(1+\frac{54Y}L\Bigr)\|G\|^2,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:A}. For $G=G_{m,m'}$, $L=4mm'$ and $Y\ge mm'$ the bracket is $\le 1+54Y/(4mm')\le 15Y/(mm')$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:norm}(i) gives \eqref{eq:A2}. \qed

\begin{remark}\label{rem:rankin}
The argument is the classical one for bounding $\sum_{n\le Y}|a(n)|^2$ by the norm (compare \cite[Chapter~5]{IwaTopics}); its only input is the invariance of $y^2|G|^2$. The strip $y_0\le y\le2y_0$ with $y_0=1/(4\pi Y)$ is chosen so that the exponential factor in Parseval's identity stays at $e^{-2}$ for all $n\le Y$. For $Y\ge L$ the factor $Y^2/L$ in \eqref{eq:A} is of the correct order of magnitude: by the Rankin--Selberg method, $\sum_{n\le Y}|a(n)|^2/n\sim 16\pi^2\|G\|^2Y/\vol(\Gamma_0(L)\backslash\HH)$ with $\vol=\frac\pi3L\prod_{p\mid L}(1+1/p)$ (measure $\dd\mu$), so that $\sum_{n\le Y}|a(n)|^2\sim 8\pi^2\|G\|^2Y^2/\vol$ for fixed $G$ as $Y\to\infty$. Theorem~\ref{thm:A} makes this uniform in the level, at the cost of the constant.
\end{remark}

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B} and Corollary~\ref{cor:C}}\label{sec:B}

\subsection{The remainder on average}
We follow the proof of Theorem~3.2 of Part~I, replacing the uniform bound for $|T-M|$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:A} after an application of Cauchy--Schwarz over $N$. Recall from Part~I, (16), that for $N\equiv2\pmod4$ and coprime square-free $d,e$ with $(de,2N)=1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rdecomp}
R(d,e;N)=\sum_{t\mid N^\flat}\mu(t)\sum_{m\mid d^2}\sum_{m'\mid e^2}c_d(m)c_e(m')\sum_{\substack{t_1\mid t^2\\ t\mid t_1}}c_t(t_1)\,\bigl(a_{G}(N)-a_{G}(N/2)\bigr),\qquad G=G_{t_1m,m'},
\end{equation}
where $N^\flat$ is the product of the odd primes dividing $N$, $m=ds$, $m'=es'$ with $s\mid d$, $s'\mid e$, and likewise $t_1=tu$ with $u\mid t$ (the coefficients $c_d(m)$, $c_t(t_1)$ of Part~I, (15), vanish unless $m=ds$ with $s\mid d$, resp.\ $t_1=tu$ with $u\mid t$; in particular $t\mid t_1$); the parameters $t_1m,m'$ of the form $Q_{t_1m,m'}$ are coprime, odd and cube-free, $\tilde m\tilde m':=t_1mm'=t_1ss'\cdot de$, and the coefficients $c$ satisfy $|c_d(m)c_e(m')c_t(t_1)|\le3^{\omega(d)+\omega(e)+\omega(t)}$. (For the reader's convenience: $c_d$ is the multiplicative coefficient of Part~I, (15), with $c_p(p)=2$, $c_p(p^2)=-1$ for a split prime $p$ and $c_q(q)=0$, $c_q(q^2)=1$ for an inert prime $q$; it encodes the identities $r(n)=2r(n/p)-r(n/p^2)$ and $r(n)=r(n/q^2)$, which turn the condition $d\mid n$ into a combination of theta series $\theta^2(mz)$. The sum over $t$ is the M\"obius inversion of the condition $(n,N)=1$, and $a_G(N)-a_G(N/2)$ selects the odd $n$.) Let $\eta=\e/10$. For $N\le X$ with $X\ge X_0(\e)$ we have, as in (F1)--(F3) of Part~I: $d_3(de)\,3^{\omega(d)+\omega(e)+\omega(t)}\,4^{\omega(N)}d(N)^2\le X^\eta$; for fixed $(d,e,N)$ the number of tuples $(s,s',t,t_1)$ is at most $X^\eta$; and $\sum_{k\le D}d(k)/k\le X^\eta$, $\sum_{k\le D}d(k)k^{-1/2}\le D^{1/2}X^{\eta}$. We also use the two bounds for a single term,
\begin{gather}
|a_G(N')|\le T(\tilde m,\tilde m';N')+M(\tilde m,\tilde m';N')\ll X^{2\eta}\Bigl(\frac{X}{\tilde m\tilde m'}+1\Bigr)\qquad(N'\le X),\label{eq:triv}\\
\sum_{n\le X}|a_G(n)|\le X^{1/2}\Bigl(\sum_{n\le X}|a_G(n)|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\ll X\Bigl(1+\frac XL\Bigr)^{1/2}L^{\eta},\label{eq:ms}
\end{gather}
where $G=G_{\tilde m,\tilde m'}$ and $L=4\tilde m\tilde m'$, the first being (21) of Part~I, the second Cauchy--Schwarz, \eqref{eq:A} and Theorem~\ref{thm:norm}(i); for $L\le X$ the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:ms} is $\ll X^{3/2}(\tilde m\tilde m')^{-1/2+\eta}$. Let $\Sigma$ denote the double sum in Theorem~\ref{thm:B}. Inserting \eqref{eq:Rdecomp} and splitting the tuples according to the size of $t_1ss'$:

\emph{Tuples with $t_1ss'>X^\e$.} For each $N$ and each tuple, \eqref{eq:triv} with $\tilde m\tilde m'>X^\e de$ gives $|a_G(N')|\ll X^{2\eta}(X^{1-\e}/(de)+1)$. Summing over the $\le X^\eta$ tuples, over $N\le X$ and over $de\le D$ with the weights $d_3(de)\cdot3^{\omega}\le X^\eta$, these tuples contribute
\[
\ll X^{1+4\eta}\sum_{k\le D}d(k)\Bigl(\frac{X^{1-\e}}{k}+1\Bigr)\ll X^{2-\e+5\eta}+X^{1+5\eta}D\ll X^{2-\e/2}.
\]

\emph{Tuples with $t_1ss'\le X^\e$.} Now $t\le t_1\le X^\e$ (as $t\mid t_1$), so $t$ runs over at most $X^\e$ values, $t_1$ over at most $d(t^2)\le X^\eta$ values, and $(s,s')$ over at most $d(d)d(e)\le X^\eta$ values; the forms $G=G_{t_1m,m'}$ have level $L=4t_1ss'de\le4X^\e D\le X$, so that the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:ms} is $\ll X^{3/2}(\tilde m\tilde m')^{-1/2+\eta}$. For a fixed tuple and fixed $(d,e)$, we drop the condition $t\mid N$ except for the count: the number of $N\le X$ with $t\mid N$ is $\le X/t$, and Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\begin{align*}
\sum_{\substack{N\le X\\ t\mid N}}\bigl(|a_G(N)|+|a_G(N/2)|\bigr)&\le2\Bigl(\frac Xt\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigl(\sum_{n\le X}|a_G(n)|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\\
&\ll\Bigl(\frac Xt\Bigr)^{1/2}X\,(\tilde m\tilde m')^{-1/2+\eta}\le X^{3/2+\eta}\,t^{-1/2}(de)^{-1/2},
\end{align*}
using \eqref{eq:A} in the form $\sum_{n\le X}|a_G(n)|^2\ll X^2\|G\|^2/L$ and $(\tilde m\tilde m')^{-1/2+\eta}\le X^{\eta}(de)^{-1/2}$. Summing over $t\le X^\e$ ($\sum_t t^{-1/2}\le2X^{\e/2}$), over the other tuple variables ($\le X^{2\eta}$), and over $de\le D$ with the weights $\le X^\eta$,
\[
\ll X^{3/2+4\eta+\e/2}\sum_{k\le D}d(k)k^{-1/2}\ll X^{3/2+5\eta+\e/2}D^{1/2}=X^{2-\e+5\eta}=X^{2-\e/2},
\]
since $D^{1/2}=X^{1/2-3\e/2}$. Both contributions are $\ll X^{2-\e/2}$, which proves the first assertion. (The margin is small but positive: the choice $D=X^{1-3\e}$ saves $X^{-3\e/2}$ in $D^{1/2}$, of which the sum over $t$ costs $X^{\e/2}$ and the divisor-type factors cost $X^{5\eta}=X^{\e/2}$; this is why $\eta=\e/10$.)

For the second, let $S(N)$ be the inner sum with $D=X^{1-3\e}$. The number of $N\in(X/2,X]$, $N\equiv2\pmod 4$, with $S(N)>X^{1-\e/4}$ is at most $\Sigma/X^{1-\e/4}\ll X^{1-\e/4}$. For the remaining $N$, since $N^{1-3\e}\le D$ and $X^{1-\e/4}\le(2N)^{1-\e/4}\le N^{1-\e/5}$ for large $X$, \eqref{eq:B} holds. \qed

\begin{remark}
The exponent $1$ is the balance $X^{3/2}D^{1/2}=X^{2}$ between the mean-square bound $X^{3/2}(de)^{-1/2}$ for the sum over $N$ of one remainder and the number $D$ of moduli; with a norm bound $\|G_{m,m'}\|\ll(mm')^{\beta+\e}$ in place of Theorem~\ref{thm:norm}(i) one gets the level $1/(2(1+\beta))$ for every $N$ (Part~I, Remark~3.3) and $1/(1+2\beta)$ for almost all $N$; Waibel's $\beta=1/4$ gives $2/5$ and $2/3$, and $\beta=0$ gives $1/2$ and $1$. The exceptional set $X^{1-\e/4}$ comes from Markov's inequality applied to a first moment of $S(N)$, which in turn rests on the second moment of $a_G(N)$; a bound for a higher moment would shrink it, and we do not pursue this.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The sieve at the level $19/20$}
Proposition~4.3 of Part~I and its proof use the level of distribution only through the bound \eqref{eq:B} (there with $2/5$); they hold verbatim, for a given $N$, with $\theta=2/5$ replaced by any $\theta\in(0,1)$ for which \eqref{eq:B} holds with $N^{\theta-\e}$ in place of $N^{1-3\e}$, provided $\alpha<\beta_1<\theta$. Lemmas~4.1 and 4.2 of Part~I do not involve the level at all. Hence, for every $N$ satisfying \eqref{eq:B} with $3\e<1/20$, the conclusion of Proposition~4.3 holds with $\theta=19/20$, and the proof of Theorem~1.1 of Part~I goes through with the parameters certified for $\theta=19/20$. The script \texttt{certify\_modular.py} of Part~I, extended by the argument \texttt{19/20} in the repository of this paper, certifies in interval arithmetic
\begin{gather*}
\theta=\tfrac{19}{20},\qquad\alpha=\tfrac2{25},\qquad\lambda=\tfrac13,\qquad\beta_1=\tfrac{69}{100},\\
\tfrac1\lambda+\tfrac2{\beta_1}=\tfrac{407}{69}<6,\qquad \tfrac1\lambda+\tfrac1{\beta_1}=\tfrac{307}{69}<5,\qquad L-\lambda R\ge0.085058
\end{gather*}
(with $L\ge0.999484$, $R\le2.743278$, $\theta_A=0.4740$; the output files are listed at the end of the paper). By Lemma~4.2 of Part~I this gives $\Omega(n(N-n))\le5$ and $\max(\Omega(n),\Omega(N-n))\le4$, and the weight of each $n$ counted is $w(n)=16\cdot2^{\Omega(n(N-n))}\le2^{9}$. As in the proof of Theorem~1.1 of Part~I, the number of integers counted is at least

\bigl(0.085058+o(1)\bigr)\,2^{-9}\cdot8\,\Sg_r(N)\,c_{2h}\,\frac{N}{\alpha^2(\log N)^2}\ \ge\ 0.34\,\Sg_r(N)\frac{N}{(\log N)^2},

since $0.085058\cdot2^{-9}\cdot8\cdot1.66485\cdot(25/2)^2=0.3457\ldots$. The exceptional set is that of Theorem~\ref{thm:B} with, say, $\e=1/80$, so $\delta=1/320$ is admissible. \qed

\begin{remark}
The same sieve gives $(8,5)$ at the level $1/2$ (Part~I), $(6,4)$ at $2/3$ (certified: $\alpha=3/50$, $\lambda=1/3$, $\beta_1=51/100$, $L-\lambda R\ge0.036741$) and $(5,4)$ at $19/20$. \end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The vector sieve is not the natural sieve here]\label{rem:dim2}
The sieve of Part~I treats the two sides $n$ and $N-n$ by the vector sieve of Br\"udern and Fouvry, with two linear components of levels $N^{\theta_A}$ and $N^{\theta-\theta_A}$ and the lower bound $L=f_1(s_A)F_1(s_B)+F_1(s_A)f_1(s_B)-F_1(s_A)F_1(s_B)$. For $s_A,s_B\le3$ one has $f_1/F_1(s)=\log(s-1)$, so $L>0$ if and only if $(s_A-1)(s_B-1)>e$, and with $s=s_A+s_B$ fixed this requires $s>2+2\sqrt e=5.297\ldots$, attained at the symmetric split. The product sequence $n(N-n)$, however, is a sieve problem of dimension $2$ in the classical sense: under the weight $r(n)r(N-n)$ the density of $p\mid n(N-n)$ is $2h(p)$, $\sum_{p<z}2h(p)=2\log\log z+O(1)$, the condition $d\mid n(N-n)$ with $(n,N)=1$ splits uniquely as $d=d_1d_2$, $d_1\mid n$, $d_2\mid N-n$, and the remainder for the modulus $d$ is $\sum_{d_1d_2=d}R(d_1,d_2)$, which is exactly what Theorem~3.2 of Part~I and Theorem~\ref{thm:B} bound (this is the reason for the weight $d_3(de)$ there). The two-dimensional sieve of Diamond, Halberstam and Richert \cite{DH} applies to it directly, with the joint level $d\le N^{\theta}$ and the sieving limit $\beta_2=4.2665\ldots$ in place of $5.297$; its upper and lower functions $F_2,f_2$ should be used in place of the products of $F_1,f_1$ both in $L$ and in the weight term $R$. Section~\ref{sec:dim2} carries this out: with the sieve functions $F_2,f_2$ enclosed in interval arithmetic, the two-dimensional sieve gives $(7,5)$ in place of $(8,5)$ for every $N$ at the level $1/2$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:dim2}), $(6,4)$ at $2/3$ with a larger margin, and $(5,3)$ at $19/20$; the bound for $\max(\Omega(n),\Omega(N-n))$ comes from the same Richert weights, which see the two sides separately. For comparison, for the unweighted sequence $n(N-n)$, which has level of distribution $1$ trivially, the two-dimensional weighted sieve gives $N=P_2+P_3$ for every large even $N$ (Wang Yuan \cite{Wang}).
\end{remark}

\section{The uniform bound and the Poincar\'e series}\label{sec:spectral}

Let $L=4mm'$ and let $P_N\in S_2(\Gamma_0(L))$, $N\ge1$, be the Riesz representative of the $N$-th coefficient: the unique cusp form with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:riesz}
\langle f,P_N\rangle=\frac{a_f(N)}{4\pi N}\qquad\text{for all }f\in S_2(\Gamma_0(L)),
\end{equation}
i.e.\ $P_N=(4\pi N)^{-1}\sum_{f\in\mathcal B}\overline{a_f(N)}\,f$ for an orthonormal basis $\mathcal B$. (This is the $N$-th Poincar\'e series; for weight $2$ the series itself converges only conditionally, which is why we define $P_N$ by \eqref{eq:riesz}.) By (11) of Part~I,
\[
\|P_N\|^2=\frac{a_{P_N}(N)}{4\pi N}=\frac{1}{(4\pi N)^2}\sum_{f\in\mathcal B}|a_f(N)|^2=\frac{\Delta_L(N,N)}{4\pi N},
\]
where $\Delta_L(N,N)$ is the Petersson density. Hence, for $G=G_{m,m'}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:angle}
|a_G(N)|^2=(4\pi N)^2|\langle G,P_N\rangle|^2=4\pi N\,\Delta_L(N,N)\,\|G\|^2\cos^2\angle(G,P_N),
\end{equation}
where $\cos\angle(G,P_N)=|\langle G,P_N\rangle|/(\|G\|\,\|P_N\|)$. The identity separates the three quantities that enter any bound for $a_G(N)$: the norm $\|G\|^2$, which by Theorem~\ref{thm:norm}(i) is $\ll L^\e$; the Petersson density, which is $\le C'$ for $N\le L^{1/2}$ (Part~I, Lemma~2.2, Step~4) and $1+O(N^{1/2+\e}/L)$ in general; and the alignment $\cos^2\angle(G,P_N)\le1$. Theorem~\ref{thm:norm}(ii) is obtained from \eqref{eq:angle} by bounding the alignment trivially: after the coprime part of $N$ is moved into a Hecke operator (Part~I, Lemma~2.2), only $P_{n_2}$ with $n_2\in\{1,2\}$ occurs, where the density is bounded, and the norm does the rest. Theorem~\ref{thm:A} says what the alignment is on average: inserting \eqref{eq:angle} into \eqref{eq:A} and restricting to $N\in(Y/2,Y]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:avgangle}
\frac{1}{Y/2}\sum_{Y/2<N\le Y}\Delta_L(N,N)\cos^2\angle(G,P_N)\ \le\ 4e^2\Bigl(\frac1Y+\frac{54}L\Bigr)\ \ll\ \frac1L\qquad(Y\ge L),
\end{equation}
the value of $\cos^2$ for a unit vector in general position in the $\asymp L$-dimensional space $S_2(\Gamma_0(L))$: on average over $N$, the theta series is no more aligned with the Poincar\'e series than a random cusp form of level $L$ would be, and Theorem~\ref{thm:norm}(ii) says that no single $N$ is aligned by more than $N^\e L/\|G\|^2\gg L^{1-\e}$ times the average. Expanding in an orthogonal basis $\mathcal B^*$ of Hecke eigenforms as in Part~I, Section~5.5,
\[
\langle G,P_N\rangle=\sum_{\varphi\in\mathcal B^*}\frac{\langle G,\varphi\rangle\langle\varphi,P_N\rangle}{\|\varphi\|^2}=\frac{1}{4\pi N}\sum_{\varphi\in\mathcal B^*}\frac{\langle\Theta,\varphi\rangle\,a_\varphi(N)}{\|\varphi\|^2},
\]
so that Theorem~\ref{thm:norm}(ii) is the bound $\ll N^{1/2+\e}$ for this spectral sum, uniformly in $m,m'$; the first two versions of Part~I asked for a proof through the cancellation between the coefficients $\langle\Theta,\varphi\rangle$ and $a_\varphi(N)$. The proof in Part~I does not see this cancellation: it shows instead that $\sum_\varphi|\langle\Theta,\varphi\rangle|^2/\|\varphi\|^2=\|G\|^2$ is itself small, i.e.\ that the theta series has a small cuspidal projection to begin with, and the spectral sum is then bounded by Cauchy--Schwarz.

\subsection{The individual bound}\label{subsec:ind}
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} and the data (Section~\ref{subsec:sup}) say that the cancellation which the proof does not see is there for most $N$, and the sieve for every $N$ would profit from it for each $N$. We state the strongest form suggested by the data.

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:ind}
For coprime odd $m,m'$ and $N\ge1$ with $(N,mm')=1$ and $v_2(N)\le1$,
\[
|T(m,m';N)-M(m,m';N)|=|a_{G_{m,m'}}(N)|\ \ll_\e\ N^{1/2+\e}(mm')^{-1/2+\e}.
\]
In the terms of \eqref{eq:angle}: $\Delta_L(N,N)\cos^2\angle(G,P_N)\ll N^\e L^{-1+\e}/\|G\|^2$, i.e.\ no Poincar\'e series $P_N$ is aligned with $G$ by more than $N^\e L^\e$ times the average \eqref{eq:avgangle}.
\end{conjecture}

Conjecture~\ref{conj:ind} is the mean-square bound \eqref{eq:A2} applied to each $N$ separately; it is consistent with the trivial lower bound $|a_G(N)|\ge\mathrm{dist}(M,8\Z)$ for $N\ll L$, where $M\asymp N/(mm')\ll1$, and with the data of Section~\ref{subsec:sup}, where the supremum over $10^6$ to $4\cdot10^6$ values of $N$ exceeds the root mean square by a factor between $5$ and $10$ at every level computed. We do not know how to prove any power saving in $mm'$ for an individual $N$. In the spectral form the question is a twisted first moment $\sum_\varphi\langle\Theta,\varphi\rangle a_\varphi(N)/\|\varphi\|^2$ over the forms of level $L$ with the ``long'' twist $N\ge L$ (the sieve at the level $\theta$ uses $N\approx L^{1/\theta}$), outside the range $N\le L^{1-\delta}$ in which the Petersson formula evaluates such moments. Any saving would raise the level for every $N$:

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:beyond}
Let $0\le\eta\le\frac12$ and suppose that $|a_{G_{m,m'}}(N)|\ll_\e N^{1/2+\e}(mm')^{-\eta+\e}$ for all coprime odd $m,m'$ and all $N$ with $(N,mm')=1$, $v_2(N)\le1$. Then Theorem~3.2 of Part~I holds for every $N\equiv2\pmod 4$ with $D=N^{\theta-\e}$, $\theta=1/(2(1-\eta))$, i.e.\ $r(n)r(N-n)$ has level of distribution $1/(2(1-\eta))$. In particular, Conjecture~\ref{conj:ind} gives the level $1-\e$ for every $N$, and then the sieve of Section~\ref{sec:B} gives, for every sufficiently large $N\equiv2\pmod4$, $\Omega(n(N-n))\le5$ and $\max(\Omega(n),\Omega(N-n))\le4$ for at least $0.34\,\Sg_r(N)N/(\log N)^2$ integers $n$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We follow the proof of Theorem~3.2 of Part~I and only change the bound for one remainder. Each term of $R(d,e)$ is $\pm c\,a_{G}(N')$ for a form $G=G_{\tilde m,\tilde m'}$ with $\tilde m\tilde m'=t_1ss'\cdot de$, where $t\mid N^\flat$, $t_1\mid t^2$, and $N'\in\{N,N/2\}$ may have common factors with $\tilde m\tilde m'$ at the primes of $t$ only. Lemma~2.5 of Part~I reduces $a_G(N')$ to $\le4^{\omega(N)}d(N)$ terms $a_{G_{m_j,m_j'}}(N_j)$ with $(N_j,m_jm_j')=1$, $N_j\mid N'$ and $m_jm_j'\ge\tilde m\tilde m'/t_1^{2}\ge de/t_1^2$ (each reduction step at a prime $p$ divides the product of the coefficients by at most $p^2$, and only the primes of $t_1$ are involved). Hence, by the hypothesis with $\e/20$ in place of $\e$,
\[
|a_G(N')|\ll 4^{\omega(N)}d(N)\,N^{1/2+\e/20}\,(de)^{-\eta}\,t_1^{2\eta}\,(mm')^{\e/20}.
\]
Put $\eta'=\e/20$. For the terms with $t_1ss'\le N^\e$ this is $\ll N^{1/2+3\eta'+2\e\eta}(de)^{-\eta}$ by (F1) of Part~I, and their total is
\[
\ll N^{1/2+5\eta'+\e}\sum_{k\le D}d(k)k^{-\eta}\ll N^{1/2+6\eta'+\e}D^{1-\eta}=N^{1/2+6\eta'+\e}N^{1/2-\e(1-\eta)}\le N^{1-\e/2+6\eta'},
\]
since $D^{1-\eta}=N^{(\theta-\e)(1-\eta)}=N^{1/2-\e(1-\eta)}$ and $1-\eta\ge\frac12$. The terms with $t_1ss'>N^\e$ are bounded by the trivial bound \eqref{eq:triv} exactly as in Part~I, by $N^{1-\e+5\eta'}+N^{\theta+5\eta'}$. With $\theta\le1-\e$ (replace $\e$ by $\min(\e,1-\theta)$ if necessary) the total is $\ll N^{1-\e/2+6\eta'}\le N^{1-\e/5}$, which is the conclusion of Theorem~3.2 of Part~I with $N^{1-\e/5}$ in place of $N^{1-\e/3}$; Proposition~4.3 of Part~I needs only a power saving. The last assertion is Section~\ref{sec:B}, where the parameters at the level $19/20$ were certified for any $N$ satisfying \eqref{eq:B}.
\end{proof}

The exponent $\theta=1/(2(1-\eta))$ interpolates between the level $1/2$ of Part~I ($\eta=0$) and the level $1$ ($\eta=\frac12$); the level $2/3$, which gives $(6,4)$ for every $N$ with the certified parameters of Section~\ref{sec:B}, needs only $\eta=\frac14$, i.e.\ a saving of $(mm')^{-1/4}$ over Theorem~\ref{thm:norm}(ii), half of what the mean square \eqref{eq:A2} gives on average.

\subsection{An exact check at the level $20$}\label{subsec:exact}
The space $S_2(\Gamma_0(20))$ is one-dimensional, spanned by the newform $f_{20}=\eta(2z)^2\eta(10z)^2$ of the elliptic curve $20a1$: $y^2=x^3+x^2+4x+4$, which is $X_0(20)$ itself (modular degree $1$, Manin constant $1$). Hence $G_{1,5}=c\,f_{20}$ with $c=a_G(1)=T(1,5;1)-M(1,5;1)=4-\tfrac43=\tfrac83$ (the Siegel product at $n=1$ is $\frac{\pi^2}5\cdot\frac{25}{3\pi^2}\cdot\frac45$), and $\|G_{1,5}\|^2=\frac{64}9\|f_{20}\|^2$. The script \texttt{norm\_level20.py} computes $\|f_{20}\|^2$ in two independent ways: from the Rankin--Selberg asymptotic $\sum_{n\le y}a_f(n)^2/n\sim16\pi^2\|f\|^2y/\vol$, $\vol(\Gamma_0(20)\backslash\HH)=12\pi$, with the coefficients of the eta product up to $2^{20}$, which gives $0.08128$, $0.08128$, $0.08134$ at $y=2^{16},2^{18},2^{20}$; and from the period lattice $\Lambda$ of $20a1$, since $\varphi^*\omega=2\pi if(z)\dd z$ gives $\|f\|^2=\mathrm{covol}(\Lambda)/(4\pi^2)$ for a modular parametrisation of degree $1$. The periods $\omega_1=2.824375\ldots$ (real) and $\omega_2=2.274165\ldots i$ of $y^2=(x+1)(x^2+4)$ are computed by quadrature, the lattice $\Z\omega_1+\Z(\omega_1+\omega_2)/2$ is identified as the period lattice by recomputing $g_2=-44/3$ and $|g_3|=296/27$ from it through $E_4$ and $E_6$, and its covolume $3.2115478\ldots$ gives $\|f_{20}\|^2=0.0813495\ldots$. The two values agree to four decimals. The script also checks that $T(1,5;N)-M(1,5;N)$ as computed by the pipeline of Part~I equals $\frac83a_f(N)$ on every admissible $N\le2^{20}$, to $10^{-9}$. This validates the Siegel main term, the FFT computation, the Rankin--Selberg normalisation and the volume used in the estimate $\|G\|^2_{\mathrm{est}}$. The exact value $\|G_{1,5}\|^2=0.5785$ is below the estimate $0.74$ of Table~\ref{tab:ms}, as expected: the excluded $N$ are either multiples of $5$, where $|a_f(5n)|=|a_f(n)|$, or divisible by $16$, where $a_f=0$, so the admissible mean is biased upwards. With the main term made exact for every $N$ (Section~\ref{subsec:allcusps}), the estimate over all $N\le2^{21}$ is $0.5785$, in agreement with the exact value to four decimals.

\subsection{The empirical law}\label{subsec:law}
The last column of Table~\ref{tab:ms} shows $\|G\|^2_{\mathrm{est}}/(\log mm')^2$. For the five pairs with $10^2\le mm'\le1.1\cdot10^4$, $Y\ge50L$ and $mm'$ free of small prime factors, it lies between $0.348$ and $0.375$, with mean $0.365$, while the ratio to $(mm')^{1/2}$ falls from $0.79$ to $0.29$ and the ratio to $(mm')^{1/4}$ rises from $2.5$ to $3.2$ over the same range. Over a range of a factor $100$ in $mm'$ the fits $(\log mm')^2$ and $(mm')^{1/4}$ are not separated by the data alone, but Theorem~\ref{thm:norm}(i) excludes any power, and the heuristic of Part~I, Remark~2.13, that $\|G\|^2$ is a sum over the cusps of $\sum_{n\ll mm'}|b(n)|^2/n$ with $|b(n)|\approx r(\alpha)r(\beta)$ on $O(1)$ pairs, predicts $(\log mm')^2$ with a constant of the order of $\frac1{4\pi}\cdot2\cdot(\text{number of cusp classes with a large contribution})\approx0.3$--$0.5$. We therefore record
\[
\|G_{m,m'}\|^2\approx0.37\,(\log mm')^2\qquad(10^2\le mm'\le1.6\cdot10^4),
\]
and, for the root mean square of $(T-M)/\sqrt N$ over $N\le Y$, $Y\gg L$,
\[
\mathrm{rms}^2\approx\frac{16\pi^2\|G\|^2}{\vol(\Gamma_0(L)\backslash\HH)}\approx\frac{44\,(\log mm')^2}{mm'\prod_{p\mid 2mm'}(1+1/p)},
\]
which reproduces the column ``rms'' of Table~3 of Part~I ($0.27$ for $(101,103)$, $0.67$ for $(1,1009)$). This corrects the remark in Section~5.3 of the first two versions of Part~I that the root mean square ``decreases like $(mm')^{-1/4}$'' and that Waibel's bound ``appears to be sharp'': the decrease is like $(mm')^{-1/2}$ up to a logarithm, as Theorem~\ref{thm:A} with Theorem~\ref{thm:norm}(i) shows; version~3 of Part~I states the corrected law. Section~\ref{sec:lower} shows what the ``law'' is: the norm depends only on $mm'$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}(ii)), its leading term is $\frac1\pi(\log mm')^2$, and the constant $0.37$ is this leading term together with lower-order terms that are still large in the range of the sample; the estimates of Table~\ref{tab:ms} are themselves biased by the restriction to the admissible $N$ (Table~\ref{tab:cusps} has the unbiased values).

\emph{The distribution of $u(N)=a_G(N)/\sqrt N$.} In the spectral expansion of Corollary~\ref{cor:newmoment}, $a_G(N)=\sum_f\sum_{d}c^{(f)}_da_f(N/d)$ over the newforms $f$ of the levels dividing $L$, so $u(N)$ is a weighted sum of the normalised Hecke eigenvalues $\lambda_f(N/d)$ of $\asymp L$ forms, and a large value of $|u(N)|$ needs the eigenvalues $\lambda_f(N)$ to align over $f$. The script \texttt{coefficient\_moments.py} (\texttt{results/coefficient\_moments.txt}) computes the moments of $u(N)$ over the admissible $N\le2^{21}$ with the exact main term of Section~\ref{subsec:allcusps}. For the seven pairs of Table~\ref{tab:sup} with $L\le4.2\cdot10^4$ the kurtosis $m_4/m_2^2$ is $3.46$, $3.47$, $3.53$, $3.66$, $3.73$, $3.79$ for $(13,29)$, $(1,1009)$, $(61,67)$, $(1,101)$, $(3,5)$, $(101,103)$, against $3$ for a Gaussian, and the supremum over the $1.0$--$1.9\cdot10^6$ values is $5.5$--$8.4$ times the rms, against the $5.3$--$5.4$ that a Gaussian sample of this size would give: the tail is heavier than Gaussian but not by much. The divisor function is visible but not dominant: the conditional mean of $u(N)^2/m_2$ given $d(N)\ge64$ is $0.5$, $0.8$, $0.9$ for $(1,101)$, $(1,1009)$, $(13,29)$ and $1.5$, $1.7$ for $(61,67)$, $(101,103)$, and the $17\%$ of the $N$ with $d(N)\ge24$ carry $16$--$29\%$ of the fourth moment; the ten largest values of $|u(N)|$ occur at $N$ with $d(N)$ between $4$ and $144$. The pair $(1,5)$, where $G$ is a single newform, is different: $u(N)=\frac83\lambda_f(N)$ is multiplicative, its kurtosis is $7.25$, and $E[u^2\mid d(N)]$ decreases with $d(N)$. Since the fourth moment is only a bounded multiple of the square of the second, a fourth-moment version of Theorem~\ref{thm:B} would shrink the exceptional set from $O(X^{1-\e/4})$ to $O(X^{1-c\e})$ with a larger constant $c$ and no more: the exponent $1-\delta$ in Corollary~\ref{cor:C} is the nature of a mean-value argument, and only an individual bound (Conjecture~\ref{conj:ind}) removes the exceptional set.

\section{A lower bound for the norm: the regions above the cusps}\label{sec:lower}

Throughout this section $m,m'$ are coprime odd positive integers, $L=4mm'$, $\chi=\chi_{-4}$, and for $\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{GL}_2^+(\mathbb Q)$ and $k\in\{1,2\}$ we write $(f|_k\gamma)(z)=(\det\gamma)^{k/2}(cz+d)^{-k}f(\gamma z)$; thus scalar matrices act trivially, $f(mz)=m^{-k/2}(f|_kB_m)(z)$ with $B_m=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}m&0\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, and $(fg)|_2\gamma=(f|_1\gamma)(g|_1\gamma)$. We use $\theta^2\in M_1(\Gamma_0(4),\chi)$, i.e.\ $\theta^2|_1\gamma=\chi(d)\theta^2$ for $\gamma\in\Gamma_0(4)$, and $\theta(-1/(4z))=\sqrt{-2iz}\,\theta(z)$, which gives $\theta^2|_1\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\4&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)=-i\theta^2$.

\subsection{The Atkin--Lehner cusps}
For $Q\mid L$ with $(Q,L/Q)=1$, an Atkin--Lehner matrix is $W_Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}Qa&b\\Lc&Qd\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with $a,b,c,d\in\Z$ and $\det W_Q=Q$ \cite{AL}. It normalises $\Gamma_0(L)$; hence for $f\in M_2(\Gamma_0(L))$ the form $f|_2W_Q$ lies in $M_2(\Gamma_0(L))$ and does not depend on the choice of $W_Q$ (two choices differ by an element of $\Gamma_0(L)$ on either side), $f\mapsto f|_2W_Q$ is an isometry for the Petersson product which maps cusp forms to cusp forms, and it therefore preserves the orthogonal decomposition of $M_2(\Gamma_0(L))$ into the Eisenstein and the cuspidal subspace.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:AL}
Let $Q\in\{m,4m\}$. Then $\Theta_{m,m'}|_2W_Q=\e_Q\,\Theta_{1,mm'}$ with $|\e_Q|=1$, and consequently $E_{m,m'}|_2W_Q=\e_QE_{1,mm'}$ and $G_{m,m'}|_2W_Q=\e_QG_{1,mm'}$. The same holds with $m$ and $m'$ interchanged.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let first $Q=m$ and $W_m=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}ma&b\\Lc&md\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, $mad-4m'bc=1$. Then $B_mW_m=m\left(\begin{smallmatrix}ma&b\\4m'c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with the second factor in $\Gamma_0(4)$, so $\theta^2(m\cdot)|_1W_m=m^{-1/2}\theta^2|_1(B_mW_m)=m^{-1/2}\chi(d)\theta^2$; and $W_m=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\4m'c&md\end{smallmatrix}\right)B_m$ with the first factor in $\Gamma_0(4m')$, so $\theta^2(m'\cdot)|_1W_m=\chi(md)\,\theta^2(m'\cdot)|_1B_m=\chi(md)\,m^{1/2}\theta^2(mm'\cdot)$. Multiplying, $\Theta_{m,m'}|_2W_m=\chi(m)\,\theta^2(z)\theta^2(mm'z)=\chi(m)\Theta_{1,mm'}$.

Let now $Q=4m$ and $W_{4m}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}4ma&b\\Lc&4md\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, $4mad-m'bc=1$. Then $B_mW_{4m}=m\left(\begin{smallmatrix}4ma&b\\4m'c&4d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, and the second factor is an Atkin--Lehner matrix $W_4$ for $\Gamma_0(4)$; every such matrix is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\4&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ times an element of $\Gamma_0(4)$, so $\theta^2|_1W_4=\pm i\theta^2$ and $\theta^2(m\cdot)|_1W_{4m}=\pm i\,m^{-1/2}\theta^2$. Also $W_{4m}=W_4'B_m$ with $W_4'=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}4a&b\\4m'c&4md\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ an Atkin--Lehner matrix for $\Gamma_0(4m')$, and $B_{m'}W_4'=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}4a&m'b\\4c&4md\end{smallmatrix}\right)B_{m'}$ with an Atkin--Lehner matrix for $\Gamma_0(4)$ in front, so $\theta^2(m'\cdot)|_1W_4'=m'^{-1/2}(\pm i\theta^2)|_1B_{m'}=\pm i\,\theta^2(m'\cdot)$ and $\theta^2(m'\cdot)|_1W_{4m}=\pm i\,m^{1/2}\theta^2(mm'\cdot)$. Multiplying, $\Theta_{m,m'}|_2W_{4m}=\pm\Theta_{1,mm'}$. The assertions on $E$ and $G$ follow because $|_2W_Q$ preserves the orthogonal decomposition and $\Theta_{1,mm'}=E_{1,mm'}+G_{1,mm'}$ is that decomposition for $\Theta_{1,mm'}$ (Siegel's theorem applied to $Q_{1,mm'}$).
\end{proof}

We now use the formula of Part~I, Lemma~2.8 (Waibel's \cite[(22)]{Waibel}): with $\mu=[\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z):\Gamma_0(L)]$, $\tilde\mu=[\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z):\Gamma(L)]$, $\tilde\mu/\mu=L\varphi(L)$, $\cF$ the standard fundamental domain of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)$, $T=\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&t\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right):0\le t\le L-1\}$ and $\varrho_{a,c}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&*\\c&*\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:W1}
\|G\|^2=\frac{\mu}{\tilde\mu}\sum_{c\ (L)}\ \sum_{\substack{a\ (L)\\ (a,c,L)=1}}I(\varrho_{a,c}),\qquad I(\varrho)=\int_{\bigcup_{t\in T}t\cF}\bigl|(G|_2\varrho)(z)\bigr|^2\,\dd x\,\dd y ,
\end{equation}
for $G\in S_2(\Gamma_0(L))$, where $(a,c)$ runs over coprime representatives of the residue classes. Every term is non-negative, and $\bigcup_{t\in T}t\cF\supseteq\{-\tfrac12\le x\le L-\tfrac12,\ y\ge1\}$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:region}
Let $Q\in\{m,4m,m',4m'\}$ and $a(n)=a_{G_{1,mm'}}(n)$. The terms of \eqref{eq:W1} for $G=G_{m,m'}$ with $(c,L)=L/Q$ contribute in total at least
\[
\frac1{4\pi}\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{|a(n)|^2}{n}\,e^{-4\pi n/Q}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $(c,L)=L/Q$, $c=(L/Q)c_1$ with $(c_1,Q)=1$, and $(a,c)=1$; then $(a,L/Q)=1$, so $(aQ,(L/Q)c_1)=1$ and there are $b,d\in\Z$ with $aQd-b(L/Q)c_1=1$. The matrix $\varrho=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\(L/Q)c_1&Qd\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ is in $\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)$, has the prescribed first column, and $\varrho B_Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}Qa&b\\Lc_1&Qd\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ is an Atkin--Lehner matrix $W_Q$. Hence, by Lemma~\ref{lem:AL} and $B_Q^{-1}=Q^{-1}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\0&Q\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
\[
G_{m,m'}|_2\varrho=(G_{m,m'}|_2W_Q)|_2B_Q^{-1}=\e_Q\,G_{1,mm'}|_2\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\0&Q\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\frac{\e_Q}{Q}\,G_{1,mm'}\Bigl(\frac zQ\Bigr)=\frac{\e_Q}{Q}\sum_{n\ge1}a(n)\,e\Bigl(\frac{nz}{Q}\Bigr).
\]
This function has period $Q$ in $x$, and $Q\mid L$, so by Parseval over $-\frac12\le x\le L-\frac12$,
\[
I(\varrho)\ \ge\ \int_1^\infty\int_{-1/2}^{L-1/2}|G|_2\varrho|^2\,\dd x\,\dd y=\frac{L}{Q^2}\sum_{n\ge1}|a(n)|^2\int_1^\infty e^{-4\pi ny/Q}\,\dd y=\frac{L}{4\pi Q}\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{|a(n)|^2}{n}e^{-4\pi n/Q}.
\]
The number of pairs $(a,c)$ modulo $L$ with $(c,L)=L/Q$ and $(a,c,L)=1$, i.e.\ $(a,L/Q)=1$, is $\varphi(Q)\cdot\varphi(L/Q)\,Q=\varphi(L)Q$, and $\mu/\tilde\mu=1/(L\varphi(L))$. Multiplying gives the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}(i) and (ii)]
Part (ii): by Lemma~\ref{lem:AL} applied to each prime of $m$ in turn (or directly, with $W_m$ of level $L$), $G_{m,m'}|_2W_m=\varepsilon G_{1,mm'}$ with $|\varepsilon|=1$, and $|_2W_m$ is an isometry of $S_2(\Gamma_0(L))$. For (i): since $m,m'$ are odd and coprime with $mm'>1$, we have $m\ne m'$, $m\ne4m'$ and $m'\ne4m$, so the four values $L/Q$, $Q\in\{m,4m,m',4m'\}$, are distinct and the four sets of pairs $(a,c)$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:region} are disjoint. (When $m=1$ the values $Q=1$ and $Q=4$ occur: $W_1\in\Gamma_0(L)$, the class $(c,L)=L$ is the cusp $\infty$ itself and the expansion is that of $G_{1,m'}$; Lemmas~\ref{lem:AL} and \ref{lem:region} cover these cases as stated, since they use only the Atkin--Lehner matrices and Parseval's identity, and the four cusps are then all the Atkin--Lehner cusps of $\Gamma_0(4m')$.) As all terms of \eqref{eq:W1} are non-negative, (i) follows by adding the four lower bounds.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The sum $\sum r(n)^2/n$}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:r2}
For $Y\ge1$,
\begin{gather*}
S(Y):=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{r(n)^2}{n}\,e^{-n/Y}=2(\log Y)^2+c_1\log Y+c_2+O(Y^{-1/2}),\\
c_1=4\gamma+8\frac{L'}{L}(1,\chi)-8\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(2)+\frac43\log2=9.758\ldots,
\end{gather*}
with an absolute constant $c_2$ ($c_2=6.12\ldots$ numerically); in particular $S(Y)\ge2(\log Y)^2-C$ for $Y\ge1$. Moreover $\sum_{n\ge1}r(n)^2e^{-n/X}=4X\log X+O(X)$, so that $\sum_{n\le X}r(n)^2\ll X\log 2X$, for $X\ge1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $r(n)/4=\sum_{d\mid n}\chi(d)$ is multiplicative with $r(p^k)/4=k+1$, $\frac12(1+(-1)^k)$, $1$ for $p\equiv1,3\pmod4$ and $p=2$, the series $\sum_k(r(p^k)/4)^2x^k$ equals $(1+x)(1-x)^{-3}$, $(1-x^2)^{-1}$, $(1-x)^{-1}$ respectively, and
\[
D(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{r(n)^2}{n^s}=\frac{16\,\zeta(s)^2L(s,\chi)^2}{(1+2^{-s})\,\zeta(2s)}\qquad(\operatorname{Re}s>1).
\]
By Mellin inversion, $S(Y)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{(1)}\Gamma(w)Y^wD(1+w)\,\dd w$. We move the contour to $\operatorname{Re}w=-\frac12$: there $\zeta(2+2w)\ne0$ and $1/\zeta(2+2w)\ll\log(|w|+2)$, $\zeta(1+w)$ and $L(1+w,\chi)$ are $\ll(|w|+2)^{1/4}$, and $\Gamma(w)$ decays exponentially, so the integral on the new line is $O(Y^{-1/2})$. The only pole crossed is $w=0$, of order three. With $\Gamma(w)=w^{-1}(1-\gamma w+O(w^2))$, $Y^w=1+w\log Y+\frac12w^2(\log Y)^2+O(w^3)$ and $D(1+w)=4w^{-2}(1+a_1w+O(w^2))$, where $16\cdot\frac23\cdot L(1,\chi)^2/\zeta(2)=16\cdot\frac23\cdot\frac{\pi^2}{16}\cdot\frac6{\pi^2}=4$ and $a_1=2\gamma+2\frac{L'}{L}(1,\chi)-2\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(2)+\frac13\log2$, the residue is $2(\log Y)^2+4(a_1-\gamma)\log Y+c_2$. The numerical value uses $L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)=\gamma+2\log2+3\log\pi-4\log\Gamma(\frac14)=0.2456\ldots$ (the case $\mathbb Q(i)$ of the Chowla--Selberg formula; it also follows from the functional equation of $L(s,\chi)$, $\zeta'(0,a)=\log\Gamma(a)-\frac12\log2\pi$ for the Hurwitz zeta function and $\Gamma(\frac14)\Gamma(\frac34)=\pi\sqrt2$) and $\zeta'(2)/\zeta(2)=-0.5700\ldots$. Since $c_1>0$, $S(Y)\ge2(\log Y)^2+c_2-O(1)$ for $Y\ge1$. The last assertion is the same argument for $\frac1{2\pi i}\int\Gamma(w)X^wD(w)\dd w$ with the contour moved to $\operatorname{Re}w=\frac34$, where only the double pole at $w=1$ is crossed, and $\sum_{n\le X}r(n)^2\le e\sum_nr(n)^2e^{-n/X}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The main term for small $n$}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:dens}
Let $Q=Q_{m,m'}$ and $n\ge1$. Then $\beta_2(n)\le2$; $\beta_p(n)\le\sigma_{-1}(p^{v_p(n)})$ for $p\nmid2mm'$; and $\beta_p(n)\le7\,(\min(v_p(n),v_p(mm'))+1)$ for $p\mid mm'$, while $\beta_p(n)\le1+1/p$ for $p\mid mm'$, $p\nmid n$. Consequently, for $Y\ge1$,
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{r(n)\,M(m,m';n)}{n}\,e^{-n/Y}\ \ll\ \frac{Y}{mm'}\,\log(2Y)\,(\log\log3mm')^{C}
\]
with an absolute constant $C$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a prime $p$, $a\ge1$ and $t\in\Z$ let $N_2(t;p^a)=\#\{(x,y)\bmod p^a:\ x^2+y^2\equiv t\}$. We claim
\begin{equation}\label{eq:N2}
N_2(t;p^a)\le2\,(\min(v_p(t),a)+1)\,p^a\quad(p\text{ odd}),\qquad N_2(t;2^a)\le2^{a+1}.
\end{equation}
For odd $p$, the solutions with $(x,y)\not\equiv(0,0)\pmod p$ are non-singular and lift from $p$ to $p^a$ with multiplicity $p^{a-1}$; modulo $p$ there are $p-\chi(p)\le2p$ of them if $p\nmid t$ and $2(p-1)$ or $0$ if $p\mid t$. The solutions with $x\equiv y\equiv0\pmod p$ exist only if $p^2\mid t$ and are $p^2N_2(t/p^2;p^{a-2})$ in number for $a\ge2$. Hence $N_2(t;p^a)\le2p^a+p^2N_2(t/p^2;p^{a-2})$, and \eqref{eq:N2} follows by induction on $a$ ($N_2(t;p)\le2p$, $N_2(t;1)=1$). For $p=2$ and $a\ge3$: if $t\equiv1\pmod4$, exactly one of $x,y$ is odd, and since the odd squares modulo $2^a$ are the residues $\equiv1\pmod8$, each taken four times, while $y^2\equiv0$ or $4\pmod8$ according as $4\mid y$ or not, there are $2\cdot4\cdot2^{a-2}=2^{a+1}$ solutions; if $t\equiv2\pmod8$ both are odd and there are $4\cdot4\cdot2^{a-3}=2^{a+1}$ solutions; if $t\equiv3\pmod4$ or $t\equiv6\pmod8$ there are none; and if $4\mid t$ both are even and $N_2(t;2^a)=4N_2(t/4;2^{a-2})$, so \eqref{eq:N2} follows by induction from $N_2(t;2^a)\le2^{a+1}$ for $a\le2$, which one checks by hand.

Now $\beta_2(n)=\lim_a2^{-3a}\sum_{(z,w)\bmod2^a}N_2\bigl((n-m'(z^2+w^2))\bar m;2^a\bigr)\le\lim_a2^{-3a}\cdot2^{2a}\cdot2^{a+1}=2$, where $\bar m$ is an inverse of $m$ modulo $2^a$. For $p\nmid2mm'$ the formula $\beta_p(n)=(1-p^{-2})\sigma_{-1}(p^{v_p(n)})$ is (6) of Part~I. Let $p\mid mm'$, say $p^e\,\|\,m$ with $e\ge1$, $p\nmid m'$, and $p^k\,\|\,n$. By \eqref{eq:N2}, with $X=x^2+y^2$,
\[
\beta_p(n)=\lim_ap^{-3a}\sum_{(x,y)\bmod p^a}N_2\bigl((n-mX)\bar m';p^a\bigr)\le2\Bigl(1+\sum_{i\ge1}\operatorname{Prob}_{(x,y)}\bigl(p^i\mid n-mX\bigr)\Bigr),
\]
the probability referring to $(x,y)$ uniform modulo $p^a$ with $a\ge i$. For $i\le\min(k,e)$ the probability is $1$. If $k<e$ it is $0$ for $i>k$. If $k\ge e$ and $i>e$, then $p^i\mid n-mX$ if and only if $X\equiv n_1\pmod{p^{i-e}}$ with $n_1=(n/p^e)(m/p^e)^{-1}$, which has probability $N_2(n_1;p^{i-e})p^{-2(i-e)}\le2(i-e+1)p^{-(i-e)}$ by \eqref{eq:N2}, and $\sum_{j\ge1}2(j+1)p^{-j}\le\frac52$ for $p\ge3$. Hence $\beta_p(n)\le2(\min(k,e)+\frac72)\le7(\min(k,e)+1)$; for $k=0$ the exact value $1-\chi(p)/p$ is (7) of Part~I.

For the sum, write $M(m,m';n)=\pi^2n\,\beta(n)/(mm')$ with $\beta(n)=\prod_p\beta_p(n)$, so that the sum is $\frac{\pi^2}{mm'}\sum_nr(n)\beta(n)e^{-n/Y}$, and $\beta(n)\le2\,\sigma_{-1}(n)\prod_{p\mid mm'}(1+\tfrac1p)\prod_{p\mid(n,mm')}7(v_p(n)+1)$. Write $n=gk$ with $g$ composed of the primes of $mm'$ and $(k,mm')=1$; then $r(n)\le4d(g)d(k)$, $\sigma_{-1}(n)\le2^{\omega(g)}\sigma_{-1}(k)$, and $\sum_{k\le x}d(k)\sigma_{-1}(k)\ll x\log2x$ (write $\sigma_{-1}(k)=\sum_{u\mid k}1/u$ and sum over $u$ first). Therefore
\[
\sum_nr(n)\beta(n)e^{-n/Y}\ \ll\ \prod_{p\mid mm'}\Bigl(1+\frac1p\Bigr)\sum_g\frac{7^{\omega(g)}\,2^{\omega(g)}\,d(g)^2}{g}\,Y\log(2Y)\ \ll\ Y\log(2Y)\prod_{p\mid mm'}\Bigl(1+\frac{C}p\Bigr),
\]
since $\sum_{j\ge1}14(j+1)^2p^{-j}\le C/p$ for $p\ge3$, and $\prod_{p\mid mm'}(1+C/p)\le\exp(C\sum_{p\mid mm'}1/p)\ll(\log\log3mm')^{C}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}(iii)]
By (ii) we may replace $(m,m')$ by $(1,mm')$, and we write $n=mm'$. Apply (i) to the pair $(1,n)$ and keep only the two cusps $Q\in\{n,4n\}$ (of widths $n$ and $4n$), with $Y=Q/(4\pi)$, so that $n/(4\pi)\le Y\le n/\pi$; we may assume $n\ge4\pi$. Keep only $n'<n$ in the inner sum of (i) (we write $n'$ for the summation variable there), where $a(n)=r(n)-M(1,mm';n)$ and $M\ge0$, so that $|a(n)|^2\ge r(n)^2-2r(n)M(1,mm';n)$:
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{|a(n)|^2}{n}e^{-n/Y}\ \ge\ S(Y)-\sum_{n\ge mm'}\frac{r(n)^2}{n}e^{-n/Y}-2\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{r(n)M(1,mm';n)}{n}e^{-n/Y}.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:r2}, $S(Y)\ge2(\log Y)^2-C$, and the tail is at most
\[
\sum_{j\ge0}e^{-\pi2^j}\,\frac1{2^jmm'}\sum_{n<2^{j+1}mm'}r(n)^2\ \ll\ \sum_{j\ge0}e^{-\pi2^j}\log(2^{j+2}mm')\ \ll\ \log(2mm').
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:dens} with $(m,m')$ replaced by $(1,n)$, the last sum is at most a constant times $(Y/n)\log(2Y)(\log\log3n)^C\le\log(2n)(\log\log3n)^C$. Summing over the two $Q$ and using
\[
2\bigl(\log(n/4\pi)\bigr)^2+2\bigl(\log(n/\pi)\bigr)^2\ \ge\ 4(\log n)^2-4\log(4\pi^2)\log n,
\]
(i) gives
\[
\|G_{m,m'}\|^2=\|G_{1,n}\|^2\ \ge\ \frac1{\pi}(\log n)^2-\frac{\log(4\pi^2)}\pi\log n-C\log(2n)(\log\log3n)^C,
\]
which is (iii).
\end{proof}

The same computation at the four cusps of the pair $(m,m')$ itself gives $\frac1\pi((\log m)^2+(\log m')^2)$ in place of $\frac1\pi(\log mm')^2$: the cusp sums of the fundamental domain of $(m,m')$ depend on the factorisation, their total does not, and the pair $(1,mm')$ gives the best lower bound.

\subsection{The matching upper bound}\label{subsec:upper}
The proof of Theorem~1.2 of Part~I loses the factor $(mm')^\e$ at two places only: the representation numbers $r(S,n)$ of the forms $S=A(x_1^2+x_2^2)+B(x_3^2+x_4^2)$ at the cusps are bounded by $n^\e(1+g_0n/mm')$ through $r(\alpha)r(\beta)\le16\,d(\alpha)d(\beta)$, and the genus terms through Waibel's general bound $\rk(\gen S,n)\ll n(n,L)^{1/2}(nL)^\e/\sqrt{\det S}$. Replacing the first by the mean value of $r(\alpha)^2r(\beta)^2$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:r2}) and the second by the local densities themselves gives the true order.

\begin{theorem}[The exact order of the norm]\label{thm:upper}
There is an absolute constant $C$ such that for all coprime odd positive integers $m,m'$
\[
\|G_{m,m'}\|^2\ \le\ C\,(\log3mm')^2\,(\log\log3mm')^{C},
\]
and for every $k$ a constant $C_k$ such that $\|G_{m,m'}\|^2\le C_k(\log3mm')^2$ whenever $\omega(mm')\le k$. Consequently $\|G_{m,m'}\|^2\asymp_k(\log mm')^2$ for $mm'\to\infty$ with $\omega(mm')\le k$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:norm}(ii) holds with $(mm')^\e$ replaced by $(\log3mm')(\log\log3mm')^{C}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We follow the proof of Theorem~1.2 of Part~I (Section~2.5 there) and keep its notation: $L=4mm'$; for a class of pairs $(a,c)$ with $(c,L)=d<L$ we have $\hat d\in\{d,d/2\}$, the form $S$ with $A=\kappa(m'/d_{m'})d_m$, $B=\kappa(m/d_m)d_{m'}$, $\kappa\in\{\frac14,\frac12,1,2,4,8\}$, $AB=\kappa^2mm'$, $g:=(A,B)=\kappa g_0\le8d$ (Lemma~2.11 there), and the class contributes at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:classbound}
\frac{256}\pi\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\rk(S,n)^2+\rk(\gen S,n)^2}{n}\,e^{-\lambda n},\qquad \lambda=\lambda_d=\frac{2\sqrt3\pi\hat d}{L},
\end{equation}
to the sum of Lemma~2.8 there, while the class $d=L$ (the cusp $\infty$) contributes $O(1)$, because $|a_G(n)|\le T(m,m';n)+M(m,m';n)\ll n^3$ trivially and the exponential is $e^{-2\sqrt3\pi n}$. We use throughout
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lamAB}
\begin{gathered}
\lambda AB=\frac{\sqrt3\pi}2\,\kappa^2\hat d\ \ge\ c_0\hat d,\qquad c_0=\frac{\sqrt3\pi}{32},\qquad \frac{g}{\lambda AB}\le\frac{16}{c_0},\\
Y_A:=\frac1{\lambda A}\le\frac{3m}{d_m^2},\qquad Y_B:=\frac1{\lambda B}\le\frac{3m'}{d_{m'}^2},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
the last because $L/(\hat dA)\le2L/(dA)=8m/(d_2\kappa d_m^2)\le32m/d_m^2$ with $d=d_2d_md_{m'}$, $d_2\mid4$. From Lemma~\ref{lem:r2} and partial summation, for $Y>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rsums}
\begin{gathered}
\sum_{\alpha\ge1}\frac{r(\alpha)^2}{\alpha}e^{-\alpha/Y}\ll(1+\log^+Y)^2,\qquad \sum_{\alpha\ge1}r(\alpha)^2e^{-\alpha/Y}\ll Y(1+\log^+Y),\\
\sum_{\alpha\ge1}\frac{r(\alpha)^2}{\alpha^{1/2}}e^{-\alpha/Y}\ll Y^{1/2}(1+\log^+Y)
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
(for $Y<1$ the sums are $\ll e^{-1/(2Y)}$).

\emph{The representation numbers.} $\rk(S,n)=\sum_{A\alpha+B\beta=n}r(\alpha)r(\beta)$ has at most $1+gn/(AB)$ terms, so by Cauchy--Schwarz $\rk(S,n)^2\le(1+gn/(AB))\sum_{A\alpha+B\beta=n}r(\alpha)^2r(\beta)^2$, and
\[
\sum_n\frac{\rk(S,n)^2}n e^{-\lambda n}\le\sum_{(\alpha,\beta)\ne(0,0)}r(\alpha)^2r(\beta)^2e^{-\lambda(A\alpha+B\beta)}\Bigl(\frac1{A\alpha+B\beta}+\frac g{AB}\Bigr).
\]
The terms with $\beta=0$ give, by \eqref{eq:rsums}, $\frac1A\sum_\alpha r(\alpha)^2\alpha^{-1}e^{-\lambda A\alpha}+\frac g{AB}\sum_\alpha r(\alpha)^2e^{-\lambda A\alpha}\ll\frac1A(1+\log^+Y_A)^2+\frac{g}{\lambda A^2B}(1+\log^+Y_A)$, and likewise for $\alpha=0$. For $\alpha,\beta\ge1$ we use $A\alpha+B\beta\ge2\sqrt{AB\alpha\beta}$ and \eqref{eq:rsums}:
\begin{multline*}
\sum_{\alpha,\beta\ge1}\frac{r(\alpha)^2r(\beta)^2e^{-\lambda(A\alpha+B\beta)}}{A\alpha+B\beta}\le\frac1{2\sqrt{AB}}\sum_\alpha\frac{r(\alpha)^2e^{-\lambda A\alpha}}{\alpha^{1/2}}\sum_\beta\frac{r(\beta)^2e^{-\lambda B\beta}}{\beta^{1/2}}\\
\ll\frac{(1+\log^+Y_A)(1+\log^+Y_B)}{\lambda AB},
\end{multline*}
and $\frac g{AB}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\ge1}r(\alpha)^2r(\beta)^2e^{-\lambda(A\alpha+B\beta)}\ll\frac{g}{\lambda^2A^2B^2}(1+\log^+Y_A)(1+\log^+Y_B)$. With \eqref{eq:lamAB}, $\log^+Y_A\le\log(3m)$, $\log^+Y_B\le\log(3m')$, the whole sum is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:repbound}
\sum_n\frac{\rk(S,n)^2}n e^{-\lambda n}\ \ll\ \frac{(1+\log^+(3m/d_m^2))^2}{A}+\frac{(1+\log^+(3m'/d_{m'}^2))^2}{B}+\frac{(\log3mm')^2}{\hat d}.
\end{equation}

\emph{The genus terms.} By Siegel's theorem, $\rk(\gen S,n)=\pi^2n\,\beta_S(n)/(AB)$ with $\beta_S(n)=\prod_p\beta_p(S;n)$, since $\sqrt{\det S}=4AB$. If $p\mid g$ then $S=pS_1$ with $S_1$ integral, and counting solutions modulo $p^a$ gives $\beta_p(S;n)=p\,\beta_p(S_1;n/p)$ if $p\mid n$ and $0$ otherwise; iterating over the prime factors of $g$ (including $p=2$ when $\kappa$ is even) gives $\beta_S(n)=g\,\beta_{S'}(n/g)$ if $g\mid n$ and $0$ otherwise, where $S'=S/g=Q_{A',B'}$ with $A'=A/g$, $B'=B/g$ coprime and odd. For $S'$, Lemma~\ref{lem:dens} gives $\beta_2(S';n)\le2$, $\beta_p(S';n)\le\sigma_{-1}(p^{v_p(n)})$ for $p\nmid2A'B'$, $\beta_p(S';n)\le1+1/p$ for $p\mid A'B'$, $p\nmid n$, and $\beta_p(S';n)\le7(v_p(n)+1)$ for $p\mid(n,A'B')$. Hence $\beta_{S'}(n)^2\le4\,(\sigma(A'B')/A'B')^2f(n)$ with the multiplicative function $f$ defined by $f(p^k)=\sigma_{-1}(p^k)^2$ for $p\nmid A'B'$ and $f(p^k)=49(k+1)^2$ for $p\mid A'B'$, $k\ge1$. Since $f(p^k)\le49(k+1)^2$ for all $p$ and $f(p)=1+O(1/p)$ for $p\nmid A'B'$, the mean-value inequality for non-negative multiplicative functions \cite[Theorem~01]{HT} gives
\[
\sum_{n\le X}f(n)\ll\frac X{\log X}\prod_{p\le X}\Bigl(\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{f(p^k)}{p^k}\Bigr)\ll X\prod_{p\mid A'B'}\Bigl(1+\frac{200}p\Bigr)\ll X(\log\log3A'B')^{200}\qquad(X\ge2),
\]
so that $\sum_{n\le X}\beta_S(n)^2=g^2\sum_{n'\le X/g}\beta_{S'}(n')^2\ll gX(\log\log3mm')^{C}$, and by partial summation $\sum_nn\beta_S(n)^2e^{-\lambda n}\ll g\lambda^{-2}(\log\log3mm')^C$. Therefore, by \eqref{eq:lamAB},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:genbound}
\sum_n\frac{\rk(\gen S,n)^2}n e^{-\lambda n}=\frac{\pi^4}{(AB)^2}\sum_nn\beta_S(n)^2e^{-\lambda n}\ll\frac{g\,(\log\log3mm')^C}{(\lambda AB)^2}\ll\frac{(\log\log3mm')^C}{\hat d}.
\end{equation}

\emph{The sum over the classes.} The classes are indexed by $d_2\mid4$, $d_m\mid m$, $d_{m'}\mid m'$ (with $\kappa$ and $\hat d$ determined by the class), and $\hat d\ge d/2$. Inserting \eqref{eq:repbound} and \eqref{eq:genbound} into \eqref{eq:classbound} and summing,
\begin{gather*}
\|G_{m,m'}\|^2\ll1+\Bigl((\log3mm')^2+(\log\log3mm')^C\Bigr)\sum_{d\mid L}\frac{2}{d}\\ \qquad+(\log3m)^2\sum_{\text{classes}}\frac1A+(\log3m')^2\sum_{\text{classes}}\frac1B ,\\[2pt]
\text{where}\quad
\sum_{d\mid L}\frac1d=\frac{\sigma(L)}L\ll\log\log3L,\\
\sum_{\text{classes}}\frac1A\le\sum_{d_2\mid4}\ \sum_{d_m\mid m}\ \sum_{d_{m'}\mid m'}\frac{4d_{m'}}{m'd_m}=12\,\frac{\sigma(m)}m\,\frac{\sigma(m')}{m'},
\end{gather*}
the last being $\ll(\log\log3mm')^2$, and likewise for $B$. This proves the first bound. If $\omega(mm')\le k$, then $\sigma(mm')/mm'\le2^k$, $\sigma(L)/L\le2^{k+2}$ and $\prod_{p\mid A'B'}(1+200/p)\le201^k$, so all the $\log\log$ factors are $\le C_k$, which gives the second bound; the lower bound is Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}, and the last assertion is Part~I, Lemma~2.2 and Theorem~2.3, where the norm enters only through the Cauchy--Schwarz step, which costs exactly the factor $\|G_{m,m'}\|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The spectral second moment]\label{cor:spectral}
Let $\mathcal B^*$ be an orthogonal basis of $S_2(\Gamma_0(4mm'))$ of eigenforms of the Hecke operators $T_n$, $(n,2mm')=1$, as in Part~I, Section~5.5. Then
\[
\sum_{\varphi\in\mathcal B^*}\frac{|\langle\Theta_{m,m'},\varphi\rangle|^2}{\|\varphi\|^2}=\|G_{m,m'}\|^2\asymp_k(\log mm')^2\qquad(\omega(mm')\le k),
\]
and, for every $N$ with $(N,2mm')=1$, Theorem~\ref{thm:norm}(ii) is the bound $|\sum_\varphi\langle\Theta,\varphi\rangle a_\varphi(N)/\|\varphi\|^2|\ll d(N)N^{1/2}(\log3mm')(\log\log3mm')^C$ for the spectral sum (29) of Part~I.
\end{corollary}

The first identity is Parseval's, since $E\perp\varphi$. Part~I, Section~5.5, described the Rankin--Selberg structure of the coefficients, $\langle\Theta_{m,m'},\varphi\rangle=\mathcal E_\varphi(m,m')L(\frac12,f)L(\frac12,f\otimes\chi_{-4})$ with a factor $\mathcal E_\varphi$ built from local data, without computing it. We now compute it: for newforms the local data cancel completely.

\subsection{The Rankin--Selberg formula}\label{subsec:RS}
We write $L(s,\varphi)=\sum_ka_\varphi(k)k^{-s}$ and $L(s,\varphi\otimes\chi)=\sum_k\chi(k)a_\varphi(k)k^{-s}$ for a newform $\varphi=\sum a_\varphi(k)q^k$ of weight $2$ with $a_\varphi(1)=1$, so that $s=1$ is the centre of the critical strip, and $\eta_Q(\varphi)=\pm1$ for its Atkin--Lehner eigenvalues, $\varphi|_2W_Q=\eta_Q(\varphi)\varphi$ for $Q\,\|\,L$; recall $a_\varphi(p)=-\eta_p(\varphi)$ for $p\,\|\,L$.

\begin{theorem}[The Rankin--Selberg formula]\label{thm:RS}
Let $n$ be an odd square-free positive integer and $L=4n$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em]
\item For a newform $\varphi$ of weight $2$ and level $L$,
\[
\langle\Theta_{1,n},\varphi\rangle=\frac4{\pi^2}\,\chi(n)\,\eta_n(\varphi)\,L(1,\varphi)\,L(1,\varphi\otimes\chi).
\]
\item For a newform $f$ of weight $2$ and level $M\mid L$, $M=2^eM_1$ with $M_1$ odd, and $d\mid L/M$,
\[
\langle\Theta_{1,n},f(d\,\cdot)\rangle=\frac4{\pi^2}\,\chi(M_1)\,\eta_{M_1}(f)\,\frac{\prod_{p\mid n/M_1}(1+\chi(p))}{d}\,L(1,f)\,L(1,f\otimes\chi),
\]
where $\eta_{M_1}(f)=\prod_{p\mid M_1}\eta_p(f)$; in particular $\langle\Theta_{1,n},f(d\,\cdot)\rangle=0$ unless every prime of $n/M_1$ is $\equiv1\pmod4$, and then the factor is $2^{\omega(n/M_1)}/d$.
\item For every factorisation $n=mm'$ into coprime factors,
\[
\langle\Theta_{m,m'},f(d\,\cdot)\rangle=\chi(m)\,\eta_{(m,M_1)}(f)\,\langle\Theta_{1,n},f(d\,\cdot)\rangle;
\]
for newforms of level $L$ this reads $\langle\Theta_{m,m'},\varphi\rangle=\frac4{\pi^2}\chi(m')\eta_{m'}(\varphi)L(1,\varphi)L(1,\varphi\otimes\chi)$. In particular $|\langle\Theta_{m,m'},f(d\,\cdot)\rangle|$ does not depend on the factorisation.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Here (i) is the case $M=L$, $d=1$ of (ii), and it is proved first; (ii) needs in addition the action of the Atkin--Lehner matrices of level $L$ on the oldforms $f(dz)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ALold}
Let $f$ be a newform of level $M\mid L$, $d\mid L/M$, $Q\mid n$, and put $Q_1=(Q,M)$, $Q_2=(Q,d)$, $Q_3=Q/(Q_1Q_2)$, $d'=dQ_3/Q_2$. Then $f(d\,\cdot)|_2W_Q^{(L)}=(d'/d)\,\eta_{Q_1}(f)\,f(d'\cdot)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since the odd part of $L$ is square-free, $Q_1,Q_2,Q_3$ are pairwise coprime and $d'\mid L/M$. With $W_Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}Qa&b\\Lc&Q\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, $Q^2a\delta-Lbc=Q$, one checks
\[
B_dW_Q=Q_2\,Y\,B_{d'},\qquad Y=\begin{pmatrix}Q_1Q_2a&db/Q_2\\ (L/(dQ_3))\,c&Q_1Q_3\delta\end{pmatrix},
\]
where $Y$ is integral ($Q_2\mid d$, and $dQ_3\mid L$) with $\det Y=Q_1$, $Q_1$ divides its diagonal entries and $M$ divides $L/(dQ_3)$ because $dQ_3\mid L/M$ (including the $2$-part, as $d\mid L/M$); so $Y$ is an Atkin--Lehner matrix of level $M$ and index $Q_1$, and $f|_2Y=\eta_{Q_1}(f)f$. As $f(d\,\cdot)=d^{-1}f|_2B_d$ and scalars act trivially, $f(d\,\cdot)|_2W_Q=d^{-1}f|_2(YB_{d'})=d^{-1}\eta_{Q_1}(f)f|_2B_{d'}=(d'/d)\eta_{Q_1}(f)f(d'\cdot)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:RS}]
\emph{Reduction to $(1,n)$.} By Lemma~\ref{lem:AL}, applied to each prime of $m$ in turn, $\Theta_{m,m'}|_2W_m=\chi(m)\Theta_{1,n}$, and $|_2W_m$ is an isometry, so $\langle\Theta_{m,m'},f(d\,\cdot)\rangle=\chi(m)\langle\Theta_{1,n},f(d\,\cdot)|_2W_m\rangle=\chi(m)(d'/d)\eta_{(m,M_1)}(f)\langle\Theta_{1,n},f(d'\cdot)\rangle$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:ALold} (with $Q=m$), and (ii) for $d$ and $d'$ gives (iii); for $M=L$, $\chi(m)\chi(n)=\chi(m')$ and $\eta_m\eta_n=\eta_{m'}$.

\emph{The Eisenstein series.} For $\operatorname{Re}s>\frac12$ let
\[
E(z,s)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_\infty\backslash\Gamma_0(4)}\chi(d_\gamma)\,j(\gamma,z)^{-1}\,\operatorname{Im}(\gamma z)^s,\qquad j(\gamma,z)=cz+d,\quad\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right),
\]
where $\Gamma_\infty=\{\pm\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&t\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\}$; the terms for $\gamma$ and $-\gamma$ agree because $\chi(-1)=-1$. The series converges absolutely, $E(\gamma z,s)=\chi(d_\gamma)j(\gamma,z)E(z,s)$ for $\gamma\in\Gamma_0(4)$, and by Hecke's method (\cite[Chapter~4]{DS}) $E(z,s)$ continues analytically to a neighbourhood of $s=0$, where $E(z,0)$ is a holomorphic modular form in $M_1(\Gamma_0(4),\chi)$; its constant term is $1$, because the second term of the constant term carries the factor $\sum_{d\bmod c,\,(d,c)=1}\chi(d)=0$ for $4\mid c$. Since $\dim M_1(\Gamma_0(4),\chi)=1$ and $\theta^2$ has constant term $1$, $E(z,0)=\theta^2(z)$. Put $g(z)=\theta^2(nz)\in M_1(\Gamma_0(L),\chi)$ and
\[
I(s)=\int_{\Gamma_0(L)\backslash\HH}E(z,s)\,g(z)\overline{\varphi(z)}\,y^{2}\,\dd\mu,\qquad\dd\mu=\frac{\dd x\,\dd y}{y^2},
\]
which is analytic near $s=0$ with $I(0)=\langle\Theta_{1,n},\varphi\rangle$ (the integrand is $\Gamma_0(L)$-invariant, $E(z,s)$ has polynomial growth at the cusps uniformly for $s$ near $0$, and $\varphi$ decays exponentially).

\emph{Decomposition over the cusps.} The map $\Gamma_\infty\gamma\mapsto\gamma^{-1}\infty$ identifies $\Gamma_\infty\backslash\Gamma_0(4)$ with the $\Gamma_0(4)$-orbit of $\infty$ in $\mathbb P^1(\mathbb Q)$, and the right action of $\Gamma_0(L)$ decomposes this orbit into the $\Gamma_0(L)$-classes of cusps lying over $\infty$, which are the cusps $a/c$ with $4\mid c$, i.e.\ the classes of denominators $4n/Q$, $Q\mid n$, represented by $\mathfrak a_Q=W_Q\infty$. For each $Q$ let $\varrho_Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\(L/Q)c_1&Qd\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)$ with $W_Q=\varrho_QB_Q$ as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:region}, $\beta_Q=\varrho_Q^{-1}$, so that $\beta_Q^{-1}\infty=\mathfrak a_Q$ and $d_{\beta_Q}=a$. Then $\Gamma_\infty\backslash\Gamma_0(4)=\bigsqcup_{Q\mid n}\Gamma_\infty\backslash\Gamma_\infty\beta_Q\Gamma_0(L)$, and $\gamma'\mapsto\Gamma_\infty\beta_Q\gamma'$ identifies $\Gamma_{\mathfrak a_Q}\backslash\Gamma_0(L)$ with the $Q$-th piece, $\Gamma_{\mathfrak a_Q}=\beta_Q^{-1}\Gamma_\infty\beta_Q\cap\Gamma_0(L)$ being the stabiliser of the cusp, of width $w_Q=Q$. Writing $H(z)=g(z)\overline{\varphi(z)}y^2$, the automorphy of $g$ and $\varphi$ gives $H(z)=\chi(d_{\gamma'})^{-1}j(\gamma',z)H(\gamma'z)$ for $\gamma'\in\Gamma_0(L)$, hence
\[
\chi(d_{\beta_Q\gamma'})\,j(\beta_Q\gamma',z)^{-1}\operatorname{Im}(\beta_Q\gamma'z)^sH(z)=\chi(a)\,K_Q(\gamma'z),\quad K_Q(w)=j(\beta_Q,w)^{-1}\operatorname{Im}(\beta_Qw)^sH(w),
\]
and unfolding, $I(s)=\sum_{Q\mid n}\chi(a)\int_{\Gamma_{\mathfrak a_Q}\backslash\HH}K_Q\,\dd\mu$. With the scaling matrix $\sigma_Q=\beta_Q^{-1}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}Q^{1/2}&0\\0&Q^{-1/2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=Q^{-1/2}W_Q$ we have $\beta_Q\sigma_Qu=Qu$, $j(\beta_Q,\sigma_Qu)^{-1}=Q^{1/2}j(\sigma_Q,u)$, $\operatorname{Im}(\beta_Q\sigma_Qu)=Q\operatorname{Im}u$, and
\[
H(\sigma_Qu)=(g|_1\sigma_Q)(u)\overline{(\varphi|_2\sigma_Q)(u)}\,j(\sigma_Q,u)\overline{j(\sigma_Q,u)}^{2}|j(\sigma_Q,u)|^{-4}(\operatorname{Im}u)^2,
\]
so that all the factors $j(\sigma_Q,u)$ cancel and
\[
\int_{\Gamma_{\mathfrak a_Q}\backslash\HH}K_Q\,\dd\mu=Q^{1/2+s}\int_0^\infty\!\!\int_0^1(g|_1\sigma_Q)(u)\,\overline{(\varphi|_2\sigma_Q)(u)}\,v^{s}\,\dd u_1\,\dd v\qquad(u=u_1+iv).
\]
Here $g|_1\sigma_Q=g|_1W_Q$ and $\varphi|_2\sigma_Q=\varphi|_2W_Q$, because scalar matrices act trivially; and, exactly as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:AL}, $B_QW_Q=Q\left(\begin{smallmatrix}Qa&b\\4(n/Q)c_1&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with the second factor in $\Gamma_0(4n/Q)$, so that $g|_1W_Q=Q^{-1/2}\chi(d)\,\theta^2((n/Q)z)$. Since $Qad-4(n/Q)bc_1=1$ gives $ad\equiv Q\pmod 4$, $\chi(a)\chi(d)=\chi(Q)$, and by Parseval, writing $\varphi|_2W_Q=\sum_kc_Q(k)e(kz)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Is}
I(s)=\sum_{Q\mid n}\chi(Q)\,\Gamma(s+1)(4\pi)^{-s-1}\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{r(j)\,\overline{c_Q((n/Q)j)}}{((n/Q)j)^{s+1}}\qquad(\operatorname{Re}s>\tfrac12).
\end{equation}
For a newform $\varphi$ of level $L$, $c_Q(k)=\eta_Q(\varphi)a_\varphi(k)$, with real $a_\varphi$.

\emph{The Dirichlet series.} For $p\mid n/Q$ we have $p\mid L$, so $a_\varphi((n/Q)j)=a_\varphi(n/Q)a_\varphi(j)$ for all $j$, and $r(j)=4\sum_{d\mid j}\chi(d)$ is $4$ times the coefficient of the Eisenstein series with Satake parameters $(1,\chi(p))$; the standard computation of the Rankin--Selberg Euler product gives, for $\operatorname{Re}w>\frac32$,
\[
\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{r(j)a_\varphi(j)}{j^{w}}=4\,L(w,\varphi)L(w,\varphi\otimes\chi)\prod_{p\nmid L}\bigl(1-\chi(p)p^{1-2w}\bigr)=\frac{4\,L(w,\varphi)L(w,\varphi\otimes\chi)}{L(2w-1,\chi)\prod_{p\mid n}(1-\chi(p)p^{1-2w})}
\]
where, for $p\nmid 2L$ with $a_\varphi(p)=\alpha+\beta$, $\alpha\beta=p$ and $x=p^{-w}$, the local factor of the left-hand side is
\[
\sum_{j\ge0}\sigma_\chi(p^j)a_\varphi(p^j)x^j=\frac{1-\chi(p)p\,x^2}{(1-\alpha x)(1-\beta x)(1-\chi(p)\alpha x)(1-\chi(p)\beta x)},
\]
while at $p\mid L$, $a_\varphi(p^j)=a_\varphi(p)^j$ gives $(1-a_\varphi(p)x)^{-1}(1-\chi(p)a_\varphi(p)x)^{-1}$, the product of the two degenerate Euler factors, with no numerator. Both sides continue to $w=1$. Hence, letting $s\to0$,
\[
\langle\Theta_{1,n},\varphi\rangle=\frac1\pi\cdot\frac{L(1,\varphi)L(1,\varphi\otimes\chi)}{L(1,\chi)\prod_{p\mid n}(1-\chi(p)/p)}\sum_{Q\mid n}\chi(Q)\eta_Q(\varphi)\frac{a_\varphi(n/Q)}{n/Q}.
\]
Finally $a_\varphi(n/Q)/(n/Q)=\prod_{p\mid n/Q}(-\eta_p(\varphi)/p)$ and $\eta_Q=\prod_{p\mid Q}\eta_p$, so the last sum is
\[
\eta_n(\varphi)\sum_{Q\mid n}\chi(Q)\prod_{p\mid n/Q}\Bigl(-\frac1p\Bigr)=\eta_n(\varphi)\prod_{p\mid n}\Bigl(\chi(p)-\frac1p\Bigr)=\chi(n)\eta_n(\varphi)\prod_{p\mid n}\Bigl(1-\frac{\chi(p)}p\Bigr),
\]
and $L(1,\chi)=\pi/4$. This proves (i).

\emph{The oldforms.} Let now $\varphi=f(d\,\cdot)$ with $f$ a newform of level $M=2^eM_1$, $d=d_2d_{\mathrm{o}}$ with $d_2\mid2^{2-e}$ and $d_{\mathrm{o}}$ odd, and write $n=M_1d_{\mathrm{o}}R$ (the three factors are pairwise coprime, since $n$ is square-free and $d_{\mathrm{o}}\mid n/M_1$). For $Q\mid n$ write $Q=Q_1Q_2Q_3$ with $Q_1\mid M_1$, $Q_2\mid d_{\mathrm{o}}$, $Q_3\mid R$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:ALold}, $c_Q(k)=(d'/d)\eta_{Q_1}(f)a_f(k/d')$ for $d'\mid k$ and $0$ otherwise, with $d'=dQ_3/Q_2$. Put $Q'=n/Q$ and $\delta=d_{\mathrm{o}}/Q_2$, which divides $Q'$; then $d'\mid Q'j$ if and only if $d_2Q_3\mid j$, and for $j=d_2Q_3j_1$ we have $Q'j/d'=Q''j_1$ with $Q''=Q'/\delta=(M_1/Q_1)(R/Q_3)$, while $Q'j=Q'd_2Q_3j_1$. Hence the $Q$-th inner sum in \eqref{eq:Is} at $s=0$ is
\[
\frac{d'}{d}\,\eta_{Q_1}(f)\,\frac{4}{Q'd_2Q_3}\sum_{j_1\ge1}\frac{\sigma_\chi(d_2Q_3j_1)\,a_f(Q''j_1)}{j_1}=\frac{4\,\eta_{Q_1}(f)}{Q'Q_2d_2}\,D_Q,
\]
where
\[
D_Q:=\sum_{j_1\ge1}\frac{\sigma_\chi(d_2Q_3j_1)\,a_f(Q''j_1)}{j_1},
\]
since $d'/(dd_2Q_3)=1/(Q_2d_2)$. The series $D_Q$ (continued from $\operatorname{Re}w>\frac32$ as before) is an Euler product whose local factors differ from those of $\sum\sigma_\chi(j)a_f(j)j^{-w}$ only at the primes of $d_2Q_3$ (where the argument of $\sigma_\chi$ carries an extra factor $p$) and at the primes of $Q''$ (where the argument of $a_f$ does). With $x=p^{-w}$ and $L_p=L_p(w,f)L_p(w,f\otimes\chi)$: at $p=2$, $\sigma_\chi(2^k)=1$ and the factor is $\sum_ka_f(2^k)x^k=L_2(w,f)=L_2$; at $p\mid M_1$, $a_f(p^{k+v})=a_f(p)^{k+v}$ gives $a_f(p)^vL_p$ ($v=1$ for $p\mid M_1/Q_1$, $v=0$ otherwise); and at $p\nmid2M$, with $a_f(p)=\alpha+\beta$, $\alpha\beta=p$,
\begin{align*}
\sum_{k\ge0}\sigma_\chi(p^k)a_f(p^k)x^k&=(1-\chi(p)px^2)\,L_p,\\
\sum_{k\ge0}\sigma_\chi(p^{k+1})a_f(p^k)x^k&=\bigl(1+\chi(p)-\chi(p)a_f(p)x\bigr)\,L_p,\\
\sum_{k\ge0}\sigma_\chi(p^k)a_f(p^{k+1})x^k&=\bigl(a_f(p)-(1+\chi(p))px\bigr)\,L_p
\end{align*}
(the second from $\sigma_\chi(p^{k+1})=\sigma_\chi(p^k)+\chi(p)^{k+1}$, the third from $a_f(p^{k+1})=(\alpha^{k+2}-\beta^{k+2})/(\alpha-\beta)$ and $\sum_k\sigma_\chi(p^k)(\alpha x)^k=((1-\alpha x)(1-\chi(p)\alpha x))^{-1}$). Therefore, at $w=1$,
\[
D_Q=\frac{4\,L(1,f)L(1,f\otimes\chi)}{\pi\prod_{p\mid M_1}(1-\chi(p)/p)}\prod_{p\mid M_1/Q_1}a_f(p)\prod_{p\mid Q_3}\frac{1+\chi(p)-\chi(p)a_f(p)/p}{1-\chi(p)/p}\prod_{p\mid R/Q_3}\frac{a_f(p)-1-\chi(p)}{1-\chi(p)/p}.
\]
Inserting this into \eqref{eq:Is}, with $\Gamma(1)(4\pi)^{-1}$ and $1/(Q'Q_2)=1/((M_1/Q_1)\,d_{\mathrm{o}}\,(R/Q_3))$, the sum over $Q$ factors over the three parts of $Q$:
\begin{align*}
\langle\Theta_{1,n},f(d\,\cdot)\rangle&=\frac{4\,L(1,f)L(1,f\otimes\chi)}{\pi^2\,d_2d_{\mathrm{o}}\prod_{p\mid M_1}(1-\chi(p)/p)}
\Bigl[\sum_{Q_1\mid M_1}\chi(Q_1)\eta_{Q_1}(f)\prod_{p\mid M_1/Q_1}\frac{a_f(p)}p\Bigr]
\Bigl[\sum_{Q_2\mid d_{\mathrm{o}}}\chi(Q_2)\Bigr]\\
&\qquad\times\Bigl[\sum_{Q_3\mid R}\chi(Q_3)\prod_{p\mid Q_3}\frac{1+\chi(p)-\chi(p)a_f(p)/p}{1-\chi(p)/p}\prod_{p\mid R/Q_3}\frac{(a_f(p)-1-\chi(p))/p}{1-\chi(p)/p}\Bigr].
\end{align*}
The first bracket is $\chi(M_1)\eta_{M_1}(f)\prod_{p\mid M_1}(1-\chi(p)/p)$ as in (i); the second is $\prod_{p\mid d_{\mathrm{o}}}(1+\chi(p))$; and the third is a product over $p\mid R$ of
\[
\frac{\chi(p)\bigl(1+\chi(p)-\chi(p)a_f(p)/p\bigr)+\bigl(a_f(p)-1-\chi(p)\bigr)/p}{1-\chi(p)/p}=\frac{(1+\chi(p))(1-1/p)}{1-\chi(p)/p}=1+\chi(p),
\]
the terms with $a_f(p)$ cancelling. Since $n/M_1=d_{\mathrm{o}}R$ and $d=d_2d_{\mathrm{o}}$, this is (ii).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:RSsym}
(i) The Atkin--Lehner symmetries alone show where the coefficients can be non-zero: $\langle\Theta_{1,n},\varphi\rangle=\langle\Theta_{1,n}|W_n,\varphi|W_n\rangle=\chi(n)\eta_n(\varphi)\langle\Theta_{1,n},\varphi\rangle$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:AL} with $(m,m')=(n,1)$), and $\Theta|_2W_L=-\Theta$ (Section~\ref{subsec:allcusps}, the cusp $0$) gives $\langle\Theta,\varphi\rangle=-\eta_L(\varphi)\langle\Theta,\varphi\rangle$; so $\langle\Theta_{1,n},\varphi\rangle\ne0$ forces $\eta_n(\varphi)=\chi(n)$ and $\eta_4(\varphi)=-\chi(n)$, i.e.\ the root number $-\eta_L(\varphi)$ of $\varphi$ is $+1$. The formula is consistent with this: when $\eta_n\ne\chi(n)$ the right-hand side vanishes because one of the two central values does (root number $-1$).
(ii) The theorem was checked numerically (\texttt{test\_rs\_formula.py}, \texttt{test\_rs\_old.py}, outputs in \texttt{results/}): the $T_3$-eigencomponents of $G_{1,p}=T-M$ are isolated, their norms computed by Rankin--Selberg and the central values from the functional equation. For newforms: at the level $20$ (where $G_{1,5}=\frac83f_{20}$) the two sides agree to six decimals, $0.21693$; at the levels $52$, $116$ and $148$ (one, three and two newforms with non-zero component, the last two with Hecke eigenvalues in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{17})$; all with $\eta_p=+1$ and root number $+1$) the ratios are $0.99975$; $0.99997$, $0.99878$, $0.99760$; $1.00054$, $0.99932$. For oldforms, the component $v=c_1f+c_2f(2\,\cdot)+c_4f(4\,\cdot)$ of $G_{1,p}$ in the space of $f$ is its orthogonal projection, so $\|v\|^2=\langle\Theta,v\rangle=\frac4{\pi^2}\chi(p)\eta_p(f)L(1,f)L(1,f\otimes\chi)(c_1+c_2/2+c_4/4)$ by (ii); the ratios of the two sides are $1.00071$ for $f$ of level $11$ in $G_{1,11}$ ($c_1:c_2:c_4=1:3:4$), $1.00184$ for $f$ of level $26$ in $G_{1,13}$, $0.99873$ for $f$ of level $58$ in $G_{1,29}$, and $1.00068$, $1.00035$ for the two forms of level $74$ in $G_{1,37}$ ($c_1:c_2=1:2$ in the level-$2p$ cases), within the accuracy of the Rankin--Selberg norms. At the levels $44$ and $76$ ($p\equiv3\pmod4$) $G_{1,p}$ has no newform component, in accordance with (i) and the vanishing of $L(1,\varphi\otimes\chi)$ there.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[The second moment over the newforms of all levels]\label{cor:newmoment}
Let $n$ be odd and square-free. For a newform $f$ of weight $2$ and level $M=2^eM_1\mid4n$ let $S(f)$ be the span of the $f(d\,\cdot)$, $d\mid4n/M$, $\Gamma_f$ the Gram matrix $(\langle f(d\,\cdot),f(d'\cdot)\rangle)_{d,d'}$ and $w=(1/d)_d$. Then
\[
\|G_{1,n}\|^2=\sum_{f}\bigl\|\mathrm{pr}_{S(f)}\Theta_{1,n}\bigr\|^2=\frac{16}{\pi^4}\sum_{f}\gamma_f\,4^{\omega(n/M_1)}\,L(1,f)^2L(1,f\otimes\chi)^2,\qquad \gamma_f=w^{\mathrm T}\Gamma_f^{-1}w,
\]
the sum over the newforms $f$ of weight $2$ and level dividing $4n$ for which every prime of $n/M_1$ is $\equiv1\pmod 4$. In particular
\[
\sum_{\varphi\in H_2^{\mathrm{new}}(4n)}\frac{L(1,\varphi)^2L(1,\varphi\otimes\chi)^2}{\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle}\ \le\ \frac{\pi^4}{16}\,\|G_{1,n}\|^2\ \ll\ (\log3n)^2(\log\log3n)^{C},
\]
and, since $\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle\asymp n^{1+o(1)}$ for newforms of level $4n$, the harmonic second moment of the product $L(\frac12,\varphi)L(\frac12,\varphi\otimes\chi_{-4})$ of central values over the newforms of level $4n$ is $\ll(\log n)^{2+o(1)}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The spaces $S(f)$ are mutually orthogonal and span $S_2(\Gamma_0(4n))$; $\mathrm{pr}_{S(f)}\Theta=\sum_dc_df(d\,\cdot)$ with $\Gamma_fc=v$, $v_d=\langle\Theta,f(d\,\cdot)\rangle=\kappa_f/d$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:RS}(ii), where
\[
\kappa_f=\tfrac4{\pi^2}\chi(M_1)\eta_{M_1}(f)2^{\omega(n/M_1)}L(1,f)L(1,f\otimes\chi);
\]
hence $\|\mathrm{pr}_{S(f)}\Theta\|^2=v^{\mathrm T}\Gamma_f^{-1}v=\kappa_f^2\gamma_f$. The Gram matrices are explicit (see \cite[Section~2]{ILS}); for a newform of level $4n$, $\gamma_f=1/\langle f,f\rangle$.
\end{proof}

The two-sided bound of Corollary~\ref{cor:spectral} is therefore a statement about the second moment of $L(\frac12,f)L(\frac12,f\otimes\chi_{-4})$ over the newforms $f$ of all levels $2^eM_1$ with $M_1\mid n$, each weighted by the explicit $\gamma_f4^{\omega(n/M_1)}$, with both the upper and the lower bound obtained from the geometry of the cusps rather than from the Petersson formula.

\subsection{All cusps, numerically}\label{subsec:allcusps}
For square-free $m,m'$ the computation of Lemma~\ref{lem:region} can be carried out at every cusp. The cusps of $\Gamma_0(L)$ are then in bijection with the divisors $d$ of $L$ (one cusp of denominator $d$ for each $d$, since $(d,L/d)\le2$), and we write $d=d_2\delta$ with $d_2\mid4$, $\delta\mid mm'$, $\delta_m=(\delta,m)$, $\delta_{m'}=(\delta,m')$, and
\[
A=\delta_m\,\frac{m'}{\delta_{m'}},\qquad B=\delta_{m'}\,\frac{m}{\delta_m},\qquad AB=mm',\quad (A,B)=1 .
\]
The cusps with $d_2\in\{1,4\}$ are the images of $\infty$ under the Atkin--Lehner involutions $W_{L/d}$, their widths are $L/d=4mm'/\delta$ and $mm'/\delta$, and the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:AL}, applied prime by prime, gives $G_{m,m'}|_2W_{L/d}=\e\,G_{A,B}$ with $|\e|=1$ (the odd part of $L/d$ moves the primes of $\delta_m$ from the first block to the second and those of $\delta_{m'}$ from the second to the first; the $2$-part contributes a sign). The two cusps of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} with the pair $(1,mm')$ are $\delta\in\{m,m'\}$. At the cusp with $d_2=2$, the function $\theta^2$ is transformed into $\theta_2^2$, $\theta_2(z)=\sum_{x\text{ odd}}e(x^2z/4)$ (from $\theta^2|_1\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\2&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\theta_2^2$, which follows from the transformation formulae of $\theta_3,\theta_4,\theta_2$ under $z\mapsto-1/(4z)$), and $\Theta_{m,m'}$ into $(mm'/\delta)^{-1}\sum_nT^{\mathrm{odd}}(A,B;n)e(\delta nz/(4mm'))$, where $T^{\mathrm{odd}}$ counts the representations of $n$ by $Q_{A,B}$ with all four coordinates odd; the Eisenstein part of this expansion is given by Siegel's theorem for the coset $(1+2\Z)^4$ of $2\Z^4$ (Siegel's theorem with congruence conditions \cite{SiegelII}; this is also how the genus theta series with the weights $\eta$ enters in \cite[p.~15]{Waibel}), i.e.\ by $M^{\mathrm{odd}}(A,B;n)=\frac{\pi^2n}{AB}\cdot\frac12[n\equiv2(A+B)\ (8)]\prod_{p>2}\beta_p(n)$ (for $(A,B)=(1,1)$ this is Jacobi's formula $16\sigma(n/4)$ for the number of representations of $n\equiv4\pmod8$ as a sum of four odd squares). Integrating $|G|^2$ over the region above each cusp as in Lemma~\ref{lem:region} gives
\begin{multline}\label{eq:allcusps}
\|G_{m,m'}\|^2\ \ge\ \Lambda(m,m'):=\sum_{\delta\mid mm'}\Bigl[\frac1{4\pi}\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{|a_{G_{A,B}}(n)|^2}{n}\bigl(e^{-\pi\delta n/mm'}+e^{-4\pi\delta n/mm'}\bigr)\\
+\frac1\pi\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{|T^{\mathrm{odd}}(A,B;n)-M^{\mathrm{odd}}(A,B;n)|^2}{n}\,e^{-\pi\delta n/mm'}\Bigr],
\end{multline}
where $A,B$ depend on $\delta$ as above. The regions above all the cusps have total area equal to the sum of the widths, which is $\mu=L\prod_{p\mid L}(1+1/p)$, i.e.\ the fraction $3/\pi=0.955$ of $\vol(\Gamma_0(L)\backslash\HH)$; the rest of the fundamental domain lies below height $1$ at every cusp.

The script \texttt{cusp\_lower\_bound.py} evaluates \eqref{eq:allcusps}. It needs the main terms $M(A,B;n)$ and $M^{\mathrm{odd}}(A,B;n)$ for \emph{every} $n$, not only the admissible ones: the module \texttt{local\_densities.py} computes the densities at the primes $p\mid AB$ dividing $n$ by the recursion $\beta_p(Q_{A,B};pn')=2(1-\frac1p)[p\equiv1\ (4)]+\frac1p\beta_p(Q_{A/p,pB};n')$ for $p\mid A$ (the non-singular solutions have $(z,w)\not\equiv0$, the singular ones reduce to the form $Q_{A/p,pB}$), which gives $\beta_p(n)=(1+\frac1p)p^{-k}$ for $p\equiv3$ and $2-(1+\frac1p)p^{-k}$ for $p\equiv1\pmod4$, $k=v_p(n)$ (the recursion closes because $Q_{A/p,pB}$ is again a form of the family, with $p$ moved to the other block, and $\beta_p=1-\chi(p)/p$ when $p\nmid n$); the $2$-adic density by sorting the solutions by the parities of $(x,y)$ and $(z,w)$, which gives $\beta_2(n)=\frac14\beta_2(n/4)+\frac12[A\equiv B\ (4)][v_2(n)=2]+[A\not\equiv B\ (4)](1+\frac12[v_2(n)\ge3])$ for $v_2(n)\ge2$; and $\beta_2^{\mathrm{odd}}(n)=\frac12[n\equiv2(A+B)\ (8)]$. These formulae are checked exactly: at the levels $4$ and $12$, where there are no cusp forms, $T=M$ and $T^{\mathrm{odd}}=M^{\mathrm{odd}}$ for every $n\le2^{17}$ to $10^{-9}$, and at the level $20$, $T-M=\frac83a_{f_{20}}(n)$ for every $n\le2^{17}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:exact} had this only for the admissible $n$), while the mean square of $T^{\mathrm{odd}}-M^{\mathrm{odd}}$ grows like $Y^2$ as for a cusp form. With the exact main term the Rankin--Selberg estimate of Section~\ref{sec:num} can be taken over all $n\le Y$; this is the column $\|G\|^2$ of Table~\ref{tab:cusps}, and for $(1,5)$ it is $0.5785$, the exact value of Section~\ref{subsec:exact}.

\item For the ten pairs with $Y\ge50L$, the regions above the cusps carry between $93.8\%$ and $98.5\%$ of the norm (for $(105,143)$, with $Y/L=35$, $92.9\%$), about their share $95.5\%$ of the area: the function $y^2|G|^2$ has no excess mass in the remaining $4.5\%$ of the fundamental domain, near the elliptic points and in the ``corners'' between the cusps. \item The four cusps of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}(i) alone give $98\%$ for $m=1$, where they are all the Atkin--Lehner cusps, and about half for $mm'=pq$; the rest comes from the pair $(m,m')$ itself at the cusps with $\delta=1$ (widths $4mm'$ and $mm'$, and the cusp of denominator $2$): for $(13,29)$, $4.16+1.03$ out of $12.42$.
\item For the cusps with $\delta=1$, $T(m,m';n)$, $n\ll mm'$, is a sum of at most $1+n/mm'$ products $r(\alpha)r(\beta)$ with $m\alpha+m'\beta=n$; replacing $r(\alpha)^2,r(\beta)^2$ by their mean values $4\log\alpha$, $4\log\beta$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:r2}) gives for their Atkin--Lehner part
\[
\frac{16}{4\pi}\iint_{u,v>0}\frac{\log(m'u)\log(mv)}{u+v}\bigl(e^{-\pi(u+v)}+e^{-4\pi(u+v)}\bigr)\dd u\,\dd v=\frac5{\pi^2}\log m\log m'+O(\log mm'),
\]
since $\iint e^{-c(u+v)}(u+v)^{-1}\dd u\,\dd v=1/c$: $4.38$, $8.76$, $10.84$ against the computed $4.16$, $8.54$, $10.62$ for $(13,29)$, $(61,67)$, $(101,103)$.
\item The leading term $\frac1\pi((\log m)^2+(\log m')^2)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}(i) is $85$--$95\%$ of the right-hand side of (i) for $mm'\ge100$; the difference is the term $c_1\log Y+c_2$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:r2} at the four cusps, which is still large at $Y\approx10^2$--$10^3$.

For the pair $(1,mm')$ the leading term is $\frac1\pi(\log mm')^2$, which is the leading term of the norm itself (Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}(iii)); its ratio to $(\log mm')^2$ approaches $1/\pi$ only like $1/\pi+0.4/\log mm'$, and the constant $0.37$ of Section~\ref{subsec:law} is the value of this ratio in the range of the sample.

\subsection{The asymptotic formula}\label{subsec:asymp}
The lower bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} integrates over the regions above height $1$ at the cusps, the upper bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:upper} over the strips above height $\sqrt3/2$ which cover the fundamental domain; both are Parseval sums of the same cusp expansions, with the weights $e^{-4\pi k/Q}$ and $e^{-2\sqrt3\pi k/Q}$. Evaluating the Parseval sums at both heights by one computation gives the norm asymptotically, with an error of one power of the logarithm less than the main term.

\begin{lemma}[Two-sided Parseval]\label{lem:sandwich}
Let $G\in S_2(\Gamma_0(L))$, $h\in[\sqrt3/2,1]$, and for $\varrho=\varrho_{a,c}$ with $(c,L)=d$ write $G|_2\varrho=\sum_{k\ge1}b_\varrho(k)e(\hat dkz/L)$ as in Lemma~2.9 of Part~I. Put
\begin{gather*}
J_h(\varrho)=\int_h^\infty\!\!\int_{-1/2}^{L-1/2}|G|_2\varrho|^2\,\dd x\,\dd y=\frac{L^2}{4\pi\hat d}\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{|b_\varrho(k)|^2}{k}\,e^{-4\pi\hat dkh/L},\\
\Lambda_h(G)=\frac\mu{\tilde\mu}\sum_{c\ (L)}\ \sum_{\substack{a\ (L)\\(a,c,L)=1}}J_h(\varrho_{a,c}).
\end{gather*}
Then $\Lambda_1(G)\le\|G\|^2\le\Lambda_{\sqrt3/2}(G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In \eqref{eq:W1}, $\{-\frac12\le x\le L-\frac12,\ y\ge1\}\subseteq\bigcup_{t\in T}t\cF\subseteq\{-\frac12\le x\le L-\frac12,\ y\ge\sqrt3/2\}$, so $J_1(\varrho)\le I(\varrho)\le J_{\sqrt3/2}(\varrho)$ for every $\varrho$; the formula for $J_h$ is Parseval's identity over a full period, as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:region} and of Theorem~1.2 of Part~I.
\end{proof}

$\Lambda_h(G)$ is a sum over the classes $d\mid L$ of the pairs $(a,c)$, $(c,L)=d$, and we write $\Lambda_h(G)=\sum_{d\mid L}\Lambda_h(d)$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:sandwich}, $\Lambda_1(d)$ is the integral of $|G|^2\,\dd x\,\dd y$ over the region above height $1$ at the cusp of denominator $d$, and $\Lambda_{\sqrt3/2}(d)-\Lambda_1(d)$ bounds the part of the norm below height $1$ that the strips of this cusp cover. We now evaluate every $\Lambda_h(d)$ for the pair $(1,n)$, $n$ odd and square-free, to which Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}(ii) reduces the general case; the classes are $d=d_2\delta$ with $d_2\mid4$, $\delta\mid n$, as in Section~\ref{subsec:allcusps}, where now $A=n/\delta$, $B=\delta$.

\begin{lemma}[The cusps of denominator $2\delta$]\label{lem:oddcusp}
Let $n$ be odd and square-free, $L=4n$, $\delta\mid n$, and $\theta_2(w)=\sum_{x\text{ odd}}e(x^2w/4)$. There is $\varrho_0=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&b\\2\delta&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)$ with
\[
\Theta_{1,n}|_2\varrho_0(w)=\e\,\frac\delta n\,\theta_2(w)^2\,\theta_2\Bigl(\frac{\delta^2w}n\Bigr)^2=\e\,\frac\delta n\sum_{k\ge1}T^{\mathrm{odd}}(n/\delta,\delta;k)\,e\Bigl(\frac{\delta kw}{4n}\Bigr),\qquad|\e|=1,
\]
where $T^{\mathrm{odd}}(A,B;k)$ counts the representations of $k$ by $A(x_1^2+x_2^2)+B(x_3^2+x_4^2)$ with all $x_i$ odd; in particular $T^{\mathrm{odd}}(A,B;k)=0$ for $k<2(A+B)$, and $T^{\mathrm{odd}}(A,B;k)\le\sum_{A\alpha+B\beta=k,\ \alpha,\beta\ge2}r(\alpha)r(\beta)$. For every pair $(a,c)$ with $(c,L)=2\delta$, $J_h(\varrho_{a,c})=J_h(\varrho_0)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First, $\theta^2|_1\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\2&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\theta_2^2$: with $S=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ one has $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\2&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)=S^{-1}T^{-2}S$, and the formulae $\theta(-1/w)=\sqrt{-iw/2}\,\theta(w/4)$ and $\theta(u+\frac12)=2\theta(4u)-\theta(u)$ give $\theta(w/(2w+1))=\sqrt{-iv/2}\,\theta(v/4)$ with $v=-1/w-2$, where
\[
\theta(v/4)=\theta\Bigl(-\frac1{4w}+\frac12\Bigr)=2\theta(-1/w)-\theta(-1/(4w))=\sqrt{-2iw}\,\bigl(\theta(w/4)-\theta(w)\bigr)=\sqrt{-2iw}\,\theta_2(w)
\]
and $\sqrt{-iv/2}\sqrt{-2iw}=\pm\sqrt{2w+1}$. Since $\theta^2|_1\gamma=\chi(d_\gamma)\theta^2$ on $\Gamma_0(4)$, since every $\varrho\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)$ whose lower left entry is $\equiv2\pmod 4$ is $\gamma\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\2&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)T^j$ with $\gamma\in\Gamma_0(4)$ (the cusps of $\Gamma_0(4)$ are $\infty,0,\frac12$), and since $\theta_2(w+j)=e(j/4)\theta_2(w)$ ($x^2\equiv1\pmod 8$ for odd $x$), we get $\theta^2|_1\varrho=\e'\theta_2^2$, $|\e'|=1$, for all such $\varrho$.

Choose $b$ with $2\delta b\equiv-1\pmod{n/\delta}$ and put $d=1+2\delta b=(n/\delta)t$, $\varrho_0=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&b\\2\delta&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Then $B_n\varrho_0=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}n&nb\\2\delta&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\varrho''\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\delta&0\\0&n/\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with $\varrho''=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}n/\delta&\delta b\\2&t\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)$, so $\theta^2(n\,\cdot)|_1\varrho_0=n^{-1/2}\,(\theta^2|_1\varrho'')|_1\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\delta&0\\0&n/\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\e'\frac\delta n\,\theta_2(\delta^2w/n)^2$, while $\theta^2|_1\varrho_0=\e''\theta_2(w)^2$. Multiplying gives the first formula, and $\theta_2(w)^2=\sum_{x,y\text{ odd}}e((x^2+y^2)w/4)$ gives the expansion; $x_i$ odd forces $x_1^2+x_2^2\ge2$, $x_3^2+x_4^2\ge2$. Finally, all cusps $a/c$ with $(c,L)=2\delta$ are $\Gamma_0(L)$-equivalent (there is one cusp for each divisor of $L$, as $(d,L/d)\le2$), so $\varrho_{a,c}=\gamma\varrho_0T^j$ with $\gamma\in\Gamma_0(L)$, and $G|_2\varrho_{a,c}=(G|_2\varrho_0)(\cdot+j)$ has the same $|b(k)|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The classes]\label{prop:classes}
Let $n$ be odd and square-free, $G=G_{1,n}$, $h\in[\sqrt3/2,1]$, $\delta\mid n$, and let $\ell_\delta=\log^+(n/\delta^2)$. There is an absolute constant $C$ such that
\begin{gather*}
\Lambda_h(\delta)\ \text{and}\ \Lambda_h(4\delta)\ =\ \frac1{2\pi\delta}\,\ell_\delta^2+O\Bigl(\frac{d(\delta)}\delta\log(3n)(\log\log3n)^{C}\Bigr),\\
\Lambda_h(2\delta)\ \ll\ \frac{1}{\delta}\log(3n)(\log\log3n)^{C},
\end{gather*}
uniformly in $h$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{The Atkin--Lehner classes.} Let $d\in\{\delta,4\delta\}$, $Q=L/d\in\{4n/\delta,n/\delta\}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:AL} applied prime by prime (Section~\ref{subsec:allcusps}), $G|_2W_Q=\e G_{n/\delta,\delta}$, and the computation of Lemma~\ref{lem:region} with $h$ in place of $1$ gives, with $a(k)=a_{G_{n/\delta,\delta}}(k)=T(n/\delta,\delta;k)-M(n/\delta,\delta;k)$,
\[
\Lambda_h(d)=\frac1{4\pi}\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{|a(k)|^2}k\,e^{-\lambda k},\qquad\lambda=\frac{4\pi h}Q\in\Bigl[\frac{\pi h\delta}n,\frac{4\pi h\delta}n\Bigr].
\]
Write $A=n/\delta$, $B=\delta$ and $T(A,B;k)=r(k/\delta)[\delta\mid k]+T'(k)$, $T'(k)=\sum_{A\alpha+B\beta=k,\ \alpha\ge1}r(\alpha)r(\beta)$, so that $|a(k)|^2=r(k/\delta)^2[\delta\mid k]+R(k)$ with $|R(k)|\le2r(k/\delta)[\delta\mid k]\,(T'(k)+M(k))+2T'(k)^2+2M(k)^2$, $M=M(A,B;\cdot)$.

The main term is $\frac1{4\pi}\sum_j r(j)^2(\delta j)^{-1}e^{-\lambda\delta j}=\frac1{4\pi\delta}S(Y)$, $Y=1/(\lambda\delta)=Q/(4\pi h\delta^2)$. If $Y\ge1$, Lemma~\ref{lem:r2} gives $S(Y)=2(\log Y)^2+O(1+\log Y)=2\ell_\delta^2+O(1+\ell_\delta)$, since $\log Y=\log(n/\delta^2)+O(1)$; if $Y<1$ then $n/\delta^2<4\pi$, $S(Y)\le S(1)\ll1$ and $\ell_\delta\ll1$. Hence the main term is $\frac1{2\pi\delta}\ell_\delta^2+O((1+\log 3n)/\delta)$.

For $T'$ we use $\lambda A=4\pi hn/(\delta Q)\ge\pi h\ge\frac{\sqrt3}2\pi$, so that $\sum_{\alpha\ge1}r(\alpha)^2e^{-\lambda A\alpha}\ll1$, and $\lambda B\ge\pi h\delta^2/n$. The number of terms of $T'(k)$ is at most $1+k/(AB)=1+k/n$, so by Cauchy--Schwarz, $A\alpha+B\beta\ge A$ for $\alpha\ge1$, and \eqref{eq:rsums},
\begin{multline*}
\sum_k\frac{T'(k)^2}k e^{-\lambda k}\le\sum_{\alpha\ge1,\ \beta\ge0}r(\alpha)^2r(\beta)^2e^{-\lambda(A\alpha+B\beta)}\Bigl(\frac1A+\frac1n\Bigr)\\
\ll\frac1A\Bigl(1+\sum_{\beta\ge1}r(\beta)^2e^{-\lambda B\beta}\Bigr)\ll\frac\delta n\Bigl(1+\frac n{\delta^2}\log(3n)\Bigr)\ll\frac{\log 3n}\delta .
\end{multline*}
For the cross term, $\delta\mid k$ and $A\alpha+B\beta=k$ force $\delta\mid\alpha$ (as $(A,B)=1$), so with $k=\delta j$, $\alpha=\delta\alpha'$, $\beta=j-A\alpha'$,
\begin{multline*}
\sum_{\delta\mid k}\frac{r(k/\delta)T'(k)}ke^{-\lambda k}=\frac1\delta\sum_{\alpha'\ge1}r(\delta\alpha')\sum_{j>A\alpha'}\frac{r(j)r(j-A\alpha')}j\,e^{-\lambda\delta j}\\
\le\frac1\delta\sum_{\alpha'\ge1}\frac{r(\delta\alpha')}{A\alpha'}e^{-\lambda\delta A\alpha'/2}\sum_jr(j)^2e^{-\lambda\delta j/2},
\end{multline*}
by $e^{-\lambda\delta j}\le e^{-\lambda\delta A\alpha'/2}e^{-\lambda\delta j/2}$ for $j>A\alpha'$ and Cauchy--Schwarz in $j$ (both factors are $\le\sum_jr(j)^2e^{-\lambda\delta j/2}$ after the shift $j\mapsto j-A\alpha'$). Here $\lambda\delta A=\lambda n\ge\pi h\delta\ge\frac{\sqrt3}2\pi\delta$, $r(\delta\alpha')\le4d(\delta)d(\alpha')$, and $\sum_jr(j)^2e^{-\lambda\delta j/2}\ll(n/\delta^2)\log(3n)$, so the cross term is
\[
\ll\frac{d(\delta)}{\delta}\cdot\frac{\delta}{n}\cdot\frac n{\delta^2}\log(3n)\sum_{\alpha'\ge1}d(\alpha')e^{-\alpha'}\ll\frac{d(\delta)}{\delta}\log(3n).
\]
For the terms with $M$: $M(A,B;\delta j)=\pi^2\delta j\,\beta(\delta j)/n$, and the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:dens} applies to $\sum_jr(j)\beta(\delta j)e^{-\lambda\delta j}$ verbatim, with
\[
\beta(\delta j)\le2\,\sigma_{-1}(j)\prod_{p\mid n}\Bigl(1+\frac1p\Bigr)\prod_{p\mid(\delta j,n)}7\,(v_p(\delta j)+1),
\]
giving $\sum_{\delta\mid k}r(k/\delta)M(k)k^{-1}e^{-\lambda k}\ll\frac1n\cdot\frac n{\delta^2}\log(2n)(\log\log3n)^{C}$; and
\[
\sum_k\frac{M(k)^2}ke^{-\lambda k}=\frac{\pi^4}{n^2}\sum_kk\,\beta(k)^2e^{-\lambda k}\ll\frac{(\log\log3n)^{C}}{n^2\lambda^{2}}\ll\frac{(\log\log3n)^{C}}{\delta^2}
\]
by the genus-term computation in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:upper} (with $g=1$). Collecting, $\Lambda_h(d)=\frac1{2\pi\delta}\ell_\delta^2+O(d(\delta)\delta^{-1}\log(3n)(\log\log3n)^{C})$.

\emph{The classes $d=2\delta$.} By Lemma~\ref{lem:oddcusp}, $G|_2\varrho_0=\frac\delta n\sum_kc(k)e(\delta kw/L)$ with $\hat d=\delta$ and $c(k)=\e T^{\mathrm{odd}}(A,B;k)-c_E(k)$, where $\frac\delta nc_E(k)$ are the coefficients of $E_{1,n}|_2\varrho_0$. Parseval over a period and the count of the pairs $(a,c)$ in the class give
\begin{multline*}
\Lambda_h(2\delta)=\frac\mu{\tilde\mu}\,\varphi(L)\frac L{4\delta}\,J_h(\varrho_0)=\frac1\pi\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{|c(k)|^2}k\,e^{-\pi h\delta k/n}\\
\le\frac2\pi\sum_k\frac{T^{\mathrm{odd}}(A,B;k)^2+|c_E(k)|^2}k\,e^{-\pi h\delta k/n}.
\end{multline*}
The Eisenstein part: the proof of Lemma~2.9 of Part~I bounds the genus theta series separately, $|b_E(k)|^2\le16(\det D/\det A_Q)\,\rk(\gen S,k)^2$ with $\det D\le16d^2$, i.e.\ $|c_E(k)|^2\le64\,\rk(\gen S,k)^2$, and \eqref{eq:genbound} (whose $\lambda$ is $\asymp\pi h\delta/n$) gives $\sum_k|c_E(k)|^2k^{-1}e^{-\pi h\delta k/n}\ll(\log\log3n)^{C}/\delta$. The theta part: by Lemma~\ref{lem:oddcusp} and Cauchy--Schwarz, with at most $1+k/n$ pairs $(\alpha,\beta)$ and $A\alpha+B\beta\ge2A$,
\begin{multline*}
\sum_k\frac{T^{\mathrm{odd}}(A,B;k)^2}ke^{-\pi h\delta k/n}\le\sum_{\alpha,\beta\ge2}r(\alpha)^2r(\beta)^2e^{-\pi h\alpha-\pi h\delta^2\beta/n}\Bigl(\frac1{2A}+\frac1n\Bigr)\\
\ll\frac\delta n\sum_{\beta\ge2}r(\beta)^2e^{-\pi h\delta^2\beta/n}\ll\frac\delta n\cdot\frac n{\delta^2}\log(3n)=\frac{\log3n}\delta ,
\end{multline*}
by \eqref{eq:rsums}. This proves the bound for $\Lambda_h(2\delta)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The asymptotic formula]\label{thm:asymp}
Let $m,m'$ be coprime odd square-free positive integers, $n=mm'$. Then, with an absolute constant $C$,
\[
\|G_{m,m'}\|^2=\frac1\pi\sum_{\substack{\delta\mid n\\ \delta^2<n}}\frac1\delta\Bigl(\log\frac n{\delta^2}\Bigr)^2+O\bigl(\log(2n)(\log\log3n)^{C}\bigr).
\]
The terms with $\delta>1$ are non-negative and at most $(\log n)^2(\sigma(n)/n-1)$; in particular $\|G_{m,m'}\|^2\sim\frac1\pi(\log mm')^2$ whenever $\sum_{p\mid mm'}1/p\to0$, and for primes $p$
\[
\|G_{1,p}\|^2=\frac1\pi(\log p)^2+O(\log p).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}(ii), $\|G_{m,m'}\|=\|G_{1,n}\|$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:sandwich}, $\Lambda_1(G_{1,n})\le\|G_{1,n}\|^2\le\Lambda_{\sqrt3/2}(G_{1,n})$, and by Proposition~\ref{prop:classes}, for either height,
\[
\sum_{d\mid L}\Lambda_h(d)=\sum_{\delta\mid n}\Bigl(\frac1{\pi\delta}\ell_\delta^2+O\Bigl(\frac{d(\delta)}\delta\log(3n)(\log\log3n)^{C}\Bigr)\Bigr),
\]
where $\sum_{\delta\mid n}d(\delta)/\delta=\prod_{p\mid n}(1+2/p)\ll(\log\log3n)^2$. The main term is the sum in the theorem, since $\ell_\delta=0$ for $\delta^2\ge n$. For the last assertions, $\sum_{\delta\mid n,\ \delta>1}\delta^{-1}(\log(n/\delta^2))^2\le(\log n)^2\sum_{\delta\mid n,\ \delta>1}\delta^{-1}=(\log n)^2(\sigma(n)/n-1)$, and $\sigma(n)/n-1\le\exp(\sum_{p\mid n}1/p)-1$.
\end{proof}

The proof shows that the part of the norm below height $1$ at every cusp is at most $\Lambda_{\sqrt3/2}-\Lambda_1$, which is $O(\log(2n)(\log\log3n)^{C})$; the script \texttt{norm\_sandwich.py} (\texttt{results/norm\_sandwich.txt}) evaluates $\Lambda_1$ and $\Lambda_{\sqrt3/2}$ over all cusps. For the pairs of Table~\ref{tab:cusps} the Rankin--Selberg estimate of $\|G\|^2$ lies between the two, as it must; the part below height $1$ is $0.18$, $0.26$ for $(1,101)$, $(1,1009)$ and $0.59$, $1.29$, $1.47$ for $(13,29)$, $(61,67)$, $(101,103)$, i.e.\ about $0.04\log p$ for $(1,p)$ and $0.16\log mm'$ for $m\approx m'$, against the bounds $\Lambda_{\sqrt3/2}-\Lambda_1=0.60$, $0.92$ and $1.89$, $3.20$, $3.77$. For $(1,p)$ the bound is $0.134\log p$ to three decimals from $p=10^3$ to $10^6$ ($0.9166$, $1.2280$, $1.5400$, $1.8519$ at $p=1009$, $10007$, $100003$, $1000003$), of which the two cusps of widths $p$, $4p$ contribute $\frac2\pi\log\frac2{\sqrt3}\log p=0.092\log p$ through $S(2Y/\sqrt3)-S(Y)$ and the coefficients $k\ge p$ the rest.

\begin{corollary}[The second moment of the central values]\label{cor:asympL}
In the notation of Corollary~\ref{cor:newmoment}, for odd square-free $n$,
\[
\sum_f\gamma_f\,4^{\omega(n/M_1)}L(1,f)^2L(1,f\otimes\chi_{-4})^2=\frac{\pi^3}{16}\sum_{\substack{\delta\mid n\\ \delta^2<n}}\frac1\delta\Bigl(\log\frac n{\delta^2}\Bigr)^2+O\bigl(\log(2n)(\log\log3n)^{C}\bigr),
\]
the sum over the newforms $f$ of weight $2$ and level $2^eM_1\mid4n$. For $n=p$ prime the newforms are those of levels $p$, $2p$, $4p$, and the left-hand side is $\frac{\pi^3}{16}(\log p)^2+O(\log p)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:second}
Theorem~\ref{thm:asymp} determines the coefficient of $(\log p)^2$ and not that of $\log p$. The four cusps of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}(i) give, by Lemma~\ref{lem:r2}, $\frac1\pi(\log p)^2+\frac{c_1-2\log(4\pi^2)}{2\pi}\log p+O(1)=\frac1\pi(\log p)^2+0.383\log p+O(1)$ for their sums $\frac1{4\pi}(S(p/4\pi)+S(p/\pi))$ (item (f) of Section~\ref{subsec:allcusps}), but the correction by $M(1,p;k)$, the coefficients $k\ge p$ at these cusps, the other cusps and the part below height $1$ are each of order $\log p$ as well (the proof bounds them by $O(\log p)$ and the data show them of that order: $0.04\log p$ for the part below height $1$), so $0.383$ is the coefficient of one of several terms of size $\log p$. Numerically $\|G_{1,p}\|^2\approx\frac1\pi(\log p)^2+0.46\log p-1.5$ over the range of Table~\ref{tab:cusps} (from $p=101$ and $1009$), where $\Lambda(1,p)$ alone fits $0.42\log p-1.5$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The spectral support of $G_{1,n}$}\label{subsec:support}
Remark~\ref{rem:RSsym}(i) says that a newform $\varphi$ of level $4n$ occurs in $G_{1,n}$ only if $\eta_n(\varphi)=\chi(n)$ and $\eta_4(\varphi)=-\chi(n)$. The second condition is more restrictive than it looks, because $\eta_4$ is the same for all newforms of level $4n$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:eta4}
Let $n$ be odd. Every newform $\varphi\in S_2^{\mathrm{new}}(\Gamma_0(4n))$ has $\eta_4(\varphi)=-1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $\pi=\otimes_v\pi_v$ be the automorphic representation of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb A)$ generated by $\varphi$; its component $\pi_2$ at $2$ is an irreducible admissible representation of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Q_2)$ with trivial central character and conductor exponent $2$, and the eigenvalue $\eta_4(\varphi)$ of $W_4$ on the newvector of $\varphi$ is determined by $\pi_2$ alone (it is the local root number $\e(\frac12,\pi_2,\psi_2)$ for the standard additive character; see \cite[Section~2]{LW} for the dictionary between newforms and their local components). There is exactly one such $\pi_2$. Indeed, by the classification \cite[Chapters~4, 6 and 9]{BH}: a principal series $\pi(\mu_1,\mu_2)$ has conductor exponent $a(\mu_1)+a(\mu_2)$ and central character $\mu_1\mu_2$, so with trivial central character and $a(\mu_1)+a(\mu_2)=2$ one needs $a(\mu_1)=a(\mu_2)=1$, which is impossible because $\Z_2^\times/(1+2\Z_2)$ is trivial; a twisted Steinberg $\mathrm{St}\otimes\mu$ has conductor exponent $\max(1,2a(\mu))$, which is $2$ only if $a(\mu)=1$, again impossible; and a supercuspidal representation has conductor exponent $2$ if and only if it has depth zero, in which case it is $\pi(\xi)$, induced from a character $\xi$ of $E^\times$ of conductor exponent $1$ with $\xi\ne\xi^\sigma$, $E/\mathbb Q_2$ the unramified quadratic extension. The central character of $\pi(\xi)$ is $\xi|_{\mathbb Q_2^\times}\omega_{E/\mathbb Q_2}$, so $\xi|_{\Z_2^\times}=1$ and $\xi(2)=-1$; on $\mathcal O_E^\times/(1+2\mathcal O_E)=\mathbb F_4^\times$ the character $\xi$ is one of the two non-trivial characters of the cyclic group of order $3$, which are exchanged by $\sigma$, and $\pi(\xi)=\pi(\xi^\sigma)$. Hence all newforms of level $4n$, $n$ odd, have the same $\pi_2$ and the same $\eta_4$. For the newform $f_{20}$ of level $20$ we have $G_{1,5}=\frac83f_{20}\ne0$ (Section~\ref{subsec:exact}), so Remark~\ref{rem:RSsym}(i) gives $\eta_4(f_{20})=-\chi(5)=-1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Vanishing at the inert levels]\label{cor:inert}
Let $n$ be odd and square-free with $\chi_{-4}(n)=-1$, i.e.\ with an odd number of prime factors $\equiv3\pmod4$. Then $\langle\Theta_{1,n},\varphi\rangle=0$ for every newform $\varphi$ of weight $2$ and level $4n$: the cusp forms $G_{m,m'}$, $mm'=n$, lie in the span of the $f(d\,\cdot)$ with $f$ a newform of level dividing $2n$. Moreover $L(1,\varphi)L(1,\varphi\otimes\chi_{-4})=0$ for every newform $\varphi$ of level $4n$; precisely, $L(1,\varphi)=0$ if $\eta_n(\varphi)=-1$ and $L(1,\varphi\otimes\chi_{-4})=0$ if $\eta_n(\varphi)=+1$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By Remark~\ref{rem:RSsym}(i) and Proposition~\ref{prop:eta4}, $\langle\Theta_{1,n},\varphi\rangle\ne0$ would need $\eta_4(\varphi)=-\chi(n)=+1$. Hence the newform components of $G_{1,n}$ vanish, and those of $G_{m,m'}=\chi(m)G_{1,n}|_2W_m$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:AL}) with them, since $W_m$ preserves the new subspace. By Theorem~\ref{thm:RS}(i), $L(1,\varphi)L(1,\varphi\otimes\chi)=0$. The root number of $\varphi$ is $-\eta_4(\varphi)\eta_n(\varphi)=\eta_n(\varphi)$; if it is $-1$ then $L(1,\varphi)=0$ by the functional equation, and if it is $+1$ the vanishing falls on the twist.
\end{proof}

For $\chi(n)=+1$ the condition $\eta_4=-1$ is automatic, and the support of $G_{1,n}$ in the new subspace is $\{\varphi:\ \eta_n(\varphi)=+1,\ L(1,\varphi)L(1,\varphi\otimes\chi)\ne0\}$, with $\langle\Theta_{1,n},\varphi\rangle=\frac4{\pi^2}L(1,\varphi)L(1,\varphi\otimes\chi)$. The script \texttt{newform\_support.py} (\texttt{results/newform\_support.txt}) decomposes $G_{1,q}$ into its $T_3$-eigencomponents (Prony's method on $(T_3^kG)(1)$, with a further splitting by $T_5$ where two components share $a(3)$) and classifies them by $a(2)$, $a(4)$: for $q=11,19,23,31,43,47,59,67$ every component is old (levels $q$ and $2q$), as the corollary says, and for $q=5,13,17,29,37,41,53$ newforms of level $4q$ occur at every $q$ (one to four of them, with $\eta_q=+1$ throughout). In the old components the coefficient $a(q)$, normalised by $a(1)$, is $+1$ throughout: by Theorem~\ref{thm:RS}(ii) an oldform of a newform $f$ of level $q$ or $2q$ occurs only if $L(1,f)\ne0$, which forces the root number $+1$ and $\eta_q(f)=-1$ for level $q$, i.e.\ $a_f(q)=+1$. The corollary does not say which mechanism makes $L(1,\varphi\otimes\chi_{-4})$ vanish when $\eta_n(\varphi)=+1$; one expects the root number of the twist, which is a newform of level $16n$, to be $-1$ in this case.

The factor $\chi(m)\eta_{(m,M_1)}(f)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:RS}(iii) describes how the cusp forms of the different factorisations of $n$ are related, component by component.

\begin{proposition}[The factorisations of $n$]\label{prop:factor}
Let $n$ be odd and square-free, and for a newform $f$ of level $2^eM_1\mid4n$ let $\mathrm{pr}_f$ be the orthogonal projection onto $S(f)=\mathrm{span}\{f(d\,\cdot):\ d\mid4n/M\}$. Then for every factorisation $n=mm'$ into coprime factors
\[
\mathrm{pr}_fG_{m,m'}=\chi(m)\,\eta_{(m,M_1)}(f)\,\mathrm{pr}_fG_{1,n},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad G_{m,m'}=\sum_f\chi(m)\eta_{(m,M_1)}(f)\,\mathrm{pr}_fG_{1,n},
\]
and $\mathrm{pr}_fG_{1,n}$ is an eigenvector of every $W_m$, $m\mid n$, with eigenvalue $\eta_{(m,M_1)}(f)$. Consequently, for every $N$ and every function $w$ on the divisors of $n$,
\[
\sum_{m\mid n}w(m)\,a_{G_{m,n/m}}(N)=\sum_f\Bigl(\sum_{m\mid n}w(m)\chi(m)\eta_{(m,M_1)}(f)\Bigr)a_{\mathrm{pr}_fG_{1,n}}(N),
\]
and for $w=\chi$ the inner sum is $2^{\omega(n/M_1)}\prod_{p\mid M_1}(1+\eta_p(f))$: the sum $\sum_{m\mid n}\chi(m)a_{G_{m,n/m}}(N)$ sees only the $f$ with $\eta_p(f)=+1$ for all $p\mid M_1$, and among the newforms of level $4n$ only those with $\eta_p=+1$ for every $p\mid n$, each with the weight $2^{\omega(n)}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\mathrm{pr}_f\Theta_{m,m'}=\sum_dc_df(d\,\cdot)$ with $\Gamma_fc=v$, $v_d=\langle\Theta_{m,m'},f(d\,\cdot)\rangle$, and by Theorem~\ref{thm:RS}(iii) the vector $v$ for $(m,m')$ is $\chi(m)\eta_{(m,M_1)}(f)$ times the vector for $(1,n)$; $c$ is linear in $v$, and $\mathrm{pr}_fG=\mathrm{pr}_f\Theta$ since $E\perp S(f)$. Since $G_{m,m'}=\chi(m)G_{1,n}|_2W_m$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:AL}) and $W_m$ preserves $S(f)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:ALold}), $\mathrm{pr}_fG_{m,m'}=\chi(m)(\mathrm{pr}_fG_{1,n})|_2W_m$, which gives the eigenvector statement. The rest is the linearity of the Fourier coefficients and $\sum_{m\mid n}\eta_{(m,M_1)}(f)=\prod_{p\mid M_1}(1+\eta_p(f))\prod_{p\mid n/M_1}2$.
\end{proof}

\section{The two-dimensional sieve: $(7,5)$ for every $N$}\label{sec:dim2}

Remark~\ref{rem:dim2} observed that the product $n(N-n)$ is a sieve problem of dimension $2$, with the level of distribution $N^{1/2-\e}$ of Part~I, Theorem~3.2, and that the two-dimensional sieve of Diamond, Halberstam and Richert has the sieving limit $\beta_2=4.2665$ against $2+2\sqrt e=5.297$ for the vector sieve of Part~I. Here we carry this out.

\subsection{The sieve functions of dimension two}\label{subsec:F2}
We recall the definitions \cite{DHR}, \cite[Chapters~6 and 9]{DH}. For $\kappa\ge1$ the Ankeny--Onishi function $\sigma_\kappa$ is the continuous solution of
\[
\sigma_\kappa(s)=\frac{s^\kappa}{(2e^\gamma)^\kappa\,\Gamma(\kappa+1)}\quad(0<s\le2),\qquad \bigl(s^{-\kappa}\sigma_\kappa(s)\bigr)'=-\kappa s^{-\kappa-1}\sigma_\kappa(s-2)\quad(s>2),
\]
and the sieve functions $F_\kappa,f_\kappa$ are the solutions of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dde}
\begin{aligned}
F_\kappa(s)&=\frac1{\sigma_\kappa(s)}\ \ (0<s\le\alpha_\kappa), &\qquad \bigl(s^\kappa F_\kappa(s)\bigr)'&=\kappa s^{\kappa-1}f_\kappa(s-1)\ \ (s>\alpha_\kappa),\\
f_\kappa(s)&=0\ \ (0<s\le\beta_\kappa), &\qquad \bigl(s^\kappa f_\kappa(s)\bigr)'&=\kappa s^{\kappa-1}F_\kappa(s-1)\ \ (s>\beta_\kappa),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_\kappa,\beta_\kappa$ are the constants for which $F_\kappa(s),f_\kappa(s)\to1$; $\alpha_1=\beta_1=2$ and $F_1,f_1$ are the linear sieve functions, and $\alpha_\kappa>\beta_\kappa>2$ for $\kappa>1$. $F_\kappa$ decreases and $f_\kappa$ increases \cite[Chapter~6]{DH}, $F_\kappa=1+O(e^{-s})$, $f_\kappa=1-O(e^{-s})$, and both are Lipschitz: $|F_\kappa(s_1)-F_\kappa(s_2)|,|f_\kappa(s_1)-f_\kappa(s_2)|\le\kappa|s_2-s_1|/(s_1\sigma_\kappa(1))$ for $1\le s_1<s_2$ \cite[Lemma~6.2]{DH}. The sieve theorem \cite[Theorem~9.1]{DH}, in the form in which it is quoted in \cite[Section~4]{FK}, states, for a sequence $\cA$ with $|\cA_d|=X\omega(d)/d+R_d$, $\omega$ multiplicative with $\sum_{w\le p<z}\omega(p)\log p/p\le\kappa\log(z/w)+O(1)$ (the logarithmic form of the density condition $\Omega(\kappa)$ of \cite{DH}, which implies the product form used there by partial summation), $V(z)=\prod_{p<z}(1-\omega(p)/p)$ and $2\le z\le y$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dhr}
\begin{aligned}
S(\cA,z)&\le XV(z)\Bigl(F_\kappa\Bigl(\frac{\log y}{\log z}\Bigr)+E\Bigr)+2\sum_{\substack{m\mid P(z)\\ m<y}}4^{\omega(m)}|R_m|,\\
S(\cA,z)&\ge XV(z)\Bigl(f_\kappa\Bigl(\frac{\log y}{\log z}\Bigr)-E\Bigr)-2\sum_{\substack{m\mid P(z)\\ m<y}}4^{\omega(m)}|R_m|,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $E=O\bigl((\log\log y)^2(\log y)^{-1/(2\kappa+2)}\bigr)$.

The constants $\alpha_\kappa,\beta_\kappa$ are determined by two conditions, which we formulate with the adjoint functions $p_\kappa,q_\kappa$, the solutions of $s^\kappa p'(s)=-\kappa(s+1)^{\kappa-1}p(s+1)$ with $p(s)\sim s^{-\kappa}$ and of $s^\kappa q'(s)=\kappa(s+1)^{\kappa-1}q(s+1)$ with $q(s)\sim s^{\kappa}$ (Booker and Browning \cite{BB} use the equivalent $(up)'=\kappa(p(u)-p(u+1))$, $(uq)'=\kappa(q(u)+q(u+1))$). For $P=F_\kappa+f_\kappa$ and $Q=F_\kappa-f_\kappa$ the quantities
\begin{gather*}
\Phi_P(s)=s^\kappa P(s)p(s)+\kappa\int_{s-1}^s(u+1)^{\kappa-1}P(u)p(u+1)\,\dd u,\\
\Phi_Q(s)=s^\kappa Q(s)q(s)-\kappa\int_{s-1}^s(u+1)^{\kappa-1}Q(u)q(u+1)\,\dd u
\end{gather*}
are constant for $s\ge\max(\alpha_\kappa,\beta_\kappa)+1$, by the differential-delay equations, and $P\to2$, $Q\to0$ if and only if $\Phi_P=2$ and $\Phi_Q=0$ (a solution $Q$ of its equation which is not the sieve solution decays only like $s^{-2\kappa}$, and then $\Phi_Q\ne0$). The script \texttt{dhr\_sieve.py} solves \eqref{eq:dde} for given $(\alpha,\beta)$ on a grid of step $1/2000$, computes $p,q$ by backward integration from their asymptotic series, and solves $\Phi_P=2$, $\Phi_Q=0$ for $(\alpha,\beta)$. For $\kappa=1$ it returns $\alpha=\beta=2$ and reproduces $F_1(3)=2e^\gamma/3$, $f_1(4)=2e^\gamma\log3/4$ and $f_1(5/2)$ to six decimals. For $\kappa=2$ it returns
\[
\alpha_2=5.357727,\qquad \beta_2=4.266450,
\]
which agree to $10^{-8}$ with the values $\alpha_2=5.35772744559\ldots$, $\beta_2=4.26645028414\ldots$ computed in ball arithmetic by Booker and Browning \cite{BB} (truncated at $20$ decimals there), and with $\beta_2=4.2665$ of \cite{DHR}; also $F_2(s)=2(2e^\gamma)^2/s^2=25.378/s^2$ on $(0,2]$. Refining the grid to $1/4000$ changes $\alpha_2,\beta_2$ by $3\cdot10^{-8}$ and the values of $F_2,f_2$ below by less than $10^{-7}$. Some values:

\subsection{The weighted sieve}\label{subsec:weighted}
We keep the setting of Part~I, Section~4: $N\equiv2\pmod4$ large, $\cA=\{n\le N:\ (n,N)=1\}$ with the weights $w(n)=r(n)r(N-n)$, $X_N=\sum_{n\in\cA}w(n)$ and, for square-free $d$ with $(d,2N)=1$,
\[
\sum_{\substack{n\in\cA\\ d\mid n(N-n)}}w(n)=X_N\prod_{p\mid d}2h(p)+R_d,\qquad R_d=\sum_{d_1d_2=d}R(d_1,d_2;N),
\]
since $(n,N)=1$ forces $d\mid n(N-n)$ to split uniquely as $d_1\mid n$, $d_2\mid N-n$ (Remark~\ref{rem:dim2}); here $h(p)=(2p-1)/(p(p+1))$ for $p\equiv1$ and $h(q)=1/(q(q-1))$ for $q\equiv3\pmod4$, as in Part~I, (18). Therefore $\sum_{d\le D}d_3(d)|R_d|$ is bounded by the sum of Part~I, Theorem~3.2, which is $\ll N^{1-\e/3}$ for $D=N^{1/2-\e}$, and the same holds for the sums over $d\le D/p$ needed for the sequences $\cA_p=\{n\in\cA:\ p\mid n(N-n)\}$. The density $\omega(p)=2h(p)p$ satisfies $\sum_{p<z}2h(p)=2\log\log z+O(1)$, so the sieve has dimension $\kappa=2$ in the sense of \eqref{eq:dhr}. Let $0<a<b<\frac12$, $\lambda>0$, $z=N^a$, $y=N^b$, and consider
\[
W=\sum_{\substack{n\in\cA\\ (n(N-n),P(z))=1}}w(n)\Bigl(1-\lambda\sum_{\substack{z\le p<y\\ p\mid n(N-n)}}\Bigl(1-\frac{\log p}{\log y}\Bigr)\Bigr)=S(\cA,z)-\lambda\sum_{z\le p<y}\Bigl(1-\frac{\log p}{\log y}\Bigr)S(\cA_p,z).
\]
By \eqref{eq:dhr} with the lower function for $\cA$ at the level $N^{1/2-\e}$ and the upper function for $\cA_p$ at the level $N^{1/2-\e}/p$, and with the passage from the sum over $p$ to an integral as in Part~I, Section~4,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:W2}
W\ \ge\ X_NV(z)\bigl(L_2-\lambda R_2-o(1)\bigr),\qquad L_2=f_2\Bigl(\frac1{2a}\Bigr),\qquad R_2=2\int_a^b\Bigl(1-\frac tb\Bigr)F_2\Bigl(\frac{1/2-t}{a}\Bigr)\frac{\dd t}t
\end{equation}
(the $-\e$ in the level is absorbed by the Lipschitz continuity of $f_2,F_2$). On the other hand, as in Part~I, Lemma~4.2, the $n$ with $p^2\mid n(N-n)$ for some $p\ge z$ contribute $o(X_NV(z))$, and for the others the weight is positive only if $\Omega(n(N-n))<1/\lambda+2/b$ and $\max(\Omega(n),\Omega(N-n))<1/\lambda+1/b$, because $\sum_{p\mid n}(1-\log p/\log y)\ge\Omega(n)-\log n/\log y\ge\Omega(n)-1/b$ and likewise for $N-n$, while the terms with $p\ge y$ are $\le0$ and all prime factors are $\ge z$. Hence, exactly as in the proof of Theorem~1.1 of Part~I, if $L_2-\lambda R_2>0$, then for every large $N\equiv2\pmod4$ the number of $n\in\cA$ with $n$, $N-n$ products of distinct primes $\equiv1\pmod4$ exceeding $N^a$, $\Omega(n(N-n))\le k$ and $\max(\Omega(n),\Omega(N-n))\le k'$, where $k+1\ge1/\lambda+2/b$ and $k'+1\ge1/\lambda+1/b$, is at least
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count2}
\bigl(L_2-\lambda R_2-o(1)\bigr)\,2^{-(k+4)}\cdot8\,\Sg_r(N)\,c_{2h}\,\frac{N}{a^2(\log N)^2},
\end{equation}
with $c_{2h}=1.66485$ as there (the factor $2^{-(k+4)}$ is $1/\max w(n)$, since $r(n)=4\cdot2^{\omega(n)}$ for such $n$).

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:dim2}
For every sufficiently large $N\equiv2\pmod4$,
\begin{multline*}
\#\bigl\{1\le n<N:\ n=x^2+y^2,\ N-n=z^2+w^2,\ \Omega(n(N-n))\le7,\ \max(\Omega(n),\Omega(N-n))\le5\bigr\}\\
\ge\ 0.25\,\Sg_r(N)\frac{N}{(\log N)^2},
\end{multline*}
the integers counted being coprime to $N$ and products of distinct primes $\equiv1\pmod4$ exceeding $N^{1/18}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:cert}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dim2cond}
f_2(9)\ \ge\ 0.9995,\qquad R_2^*:=2\int_{1/18}^{2/5}\Bigl(1-\frac{5t}2\Bigr)F_2(9-18t)\,\frac{\dd t}t=2\int_{1.8}^{8}\frac{s-1.8}{7.2\,(9-s)}\,F_2(s)\,\dd s\ \le\ 2.625 .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is a computation in interval arithmetic, in the manner of Part~I, Appendix~A; the script is \texttt{certify\_dhr.py} and its output is in the repository. Its inputs are: the enclosures
\[
\alpha_2=5.35772744559446184227\ldots,\qquad \beta_2=4.26645028414864191641\ldots
\]
of \cite{BB}, computed there in ball arithmetic and correctly truncated at the $20$th decimal (adding $10^{-20}$ gives upper bounds); the closed form $s^{-2}\sigma_2(s)=A_2(4-2\log(s/2)-8/s+4/s^2)$ on $[2,4]$, $A_2=1/(2(2e^\gamma)^2)$, which follows from the delay equation of $\sigma_2$, and on $[4,\alpha_2]$ one more integration of that equation, enclosed cell by cell (the factor $u^{-3}(u-2)^2$ increases for $u<6$ and $s^{-2}\sigma_2(s)$ decreases); and the monotonicity of $F_2$ (decreasing) and $f_2$ (increasing) \cite[Lemma~6.2]{DH}, which bounds every cell integral of \eqref{eq:dde} by its endpoint values. Closed forms are evaluated in $80$-bit interval arithmetic and the recursions in IEEE doubles with an outward rounding step after each operation, on the grid of step $1/20000$. The enclosures obtained are $f_2(9)\in[0.999711,\ 0.999738]$ and $R_2^*\le2.623903$; the floating-point values $0.99972$ and $2.6238$ of Section~\ref{subsec:F2} lie inside. At $s=4.5,5,6,8$ the certified enclosures of $F_2,f_2$ have widths below $3\cdot10^{-5}$ and contain the values of the table above.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:dim2}]
Take $a=1/18$, $b=2/5$, $\lambda=1/3$ in \eqref{eq:W2}--\eqref{eq:count2}. Then $1/\lambda+2/b=8$ and $1/\lambda+1/b=5.5$, so $k=7$ and $k'=5$ are admissible, $L_2=f_2(9)$, $R_2=R_2^*$, and $L_2-\lambda R_2\ge0.9995-2.625/3=0.1245$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:cert}; then \eqref{eq:count2} gives the constant $0.1245\cdot2^{-11}\cdot8\cdot1.66485\cdot18^2=0.2623>0.25$. The remainder sums in \eqref{eq:dhr} carry the weight $4^{\omega(m)}$ in place of the $d_3(de)$ of Part~I, Theorem~3.2; the proof there bounds any such weight of size $N^{o(1)}$ by $N^{\eta}$ (its condition (F1)), so the theorem holds with this weight as well, and the sums over $m<N^{1/2-\e}/p$ for $\cA_p$ are sub-sums of the same sum.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:dim2} improves the pair $(8,5)$ of Part~I, Theorem~1.1, to $(7,5)$ from the same analytic input, the level $1/2$, and it is unconditional: the sieve functions enter only through Lemma~\ref{lem:cert}, whose inputs are the certified constants of \cite{BB} and the monotonicity of $F_2,f_2$.

\subsection{The symmetric remainder and the Atkin--Lehner projection}\label{subsec:symrem}
The remainder $R_d=\sum_{d_1d_2=d}R(d_1,d_2;N)$ of the two-dimensional sieve was bounded above by $\sum_{d_1d_2=d}|R(d_1,d_2;N)|$. The sum over the factorisations has an exact meaning, which we record here: it is the coefficient of the projection of the cusp form onto the joint $(+1)$-eigenspace of the Atkin--Lehner involutions $W_p$, $p\mid d$, so that every Hecke component on which some $W_p$, $p\mid d$, acts by $-1$ cancels exactly. Lemma~\ref{lem:AL} moves the whole factor $m$ of $\Theta_{m,m'}$ to the other side; the same computation moves any part of it.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:transport}
Let $m_1,m_2,m'$ be pairwise coprime odd positive integers, $L=4m_1m_2m'$ and $W_{m_1}$ an Atkin--Lehner matrix of level $L$. Then $\Theta_{m_1m_2,m'}|_2W_{m_1}=\chi(m_1)\Theta_{m_2,m_1m'}$, hence $G_{m_1m_2,m'}=\chi(m_1)\,G_{m_2,m_1m'}|_2W_{m_1}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $W_{m_1}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}m_1a&b\\Lc&m_1d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with $m_1ad-4m_2m'bc=1$, so that $d$ is odd. One checks $B_{m_1m_2}W_{m_1}=m_1B_{m_2}\gamma$ with $\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}m_1a&b\\4m_2m'c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_0(4m_2m')\subseteq\Gamma_0(4m_2)$, and $W_{m_1}=\gamma'B_{m_1}$ with $\gamma'=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\4m_2m'c&m_1d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_0(4m')$. Since $\theta^2(m_2\,\cdot)\in M_1(\Gamma_0(4m_2),\chi)$ and $\theta^2(m'\cdot)\in M_1(\Gamma_0(4m'),\chi)$,
\begin{gather*}
\theta^2(m_1m_2\,\cdot)|_1W_{m_1}=(m_1m_2)^{-1/2}\bigl(\theta^2|_1B_{m_2}\bigr)|_1\gamma=m_1^{-1/2}\chi(d)\,\theta^2(m_2z),\\
\theta^2(m'\cdot)|_1W_{m_1}=\chi(m_1d)\,m_1^{1/2}\theta^2(m_1m'z),
\end{gather*}
and the product is $\chi(m_1)\Theta_{m_2,m_1m'}$. The statement for $G$ follows as in Lemma~\ref{lem:AL}, since $W_{m_1}^2$ acts trivially.
\end{proof}

For odd square-free $d$, $s\mid d$ and $u\mid d$ put $u_s=u\,(u,s)$, the part of $ds$ supported on the primes of $u$, so that $u_s(d/u)_s=ds$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:symsum}
Let $d$ be odd and square-free, $s\mid d$, and $t_1$ odd with $(t_1,d)=1$. Then, with Atkin--Lehner matrices of level $4t_1ds$,
\[
\sum_{u\mid d}G_{t_1u_s,\,(d/u)_s}=G_{t_1,\,ds}\Big|_2\prod_{p\mid d}\bigl(1+\chi(p^{v_p(ds)})\,W_{p^{v_p(ds)}}\bigr).
\]
In every term of \eqref{eq:Rdecomp} the prime powers $p^{v_p(ds)}$ are $\equiv1\pmod4$, and then the right-hand side is $2^{\omega(d)}P^+_{d,s}G_{t_1,ds}$, where $P^+_{d,s}=\prod_{p\mid d}\frac12(1+W_{p^{v_p(ds)}})$ is the orthogonal projection onto the joint $(+1)$-eigenspace of the commuting involutions $W_{p^{v_p(ds)}}$, $p\mid d$. Consequently, for odd square-free $d$ with $(d,2N)=1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rdsym}
R_d=\sum_{t\mid N^\flat}\mu(t)\sum_{\substack{t_1=tu\\ u\mid t}}c_t(t_1)\sum_{s\mid d}c_d(ds)\,2^{\omega(d)}\Bigl(a_{P^+_{d,s}G_{t_1,ds}}(N)-a_{P^+_{d,s}G_{t_1,ds}}(N/2)\Bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:transport} with $(m_1,m_2,m')=(u_s,t_1,(d/u)_s)$, $G_{t_1u_s,(d/u)_s}=\chi(u_s)G_{t_1,ds}|_2W_{u_s}$; for coprime exact divisors $Q,Q'$ of the level $W_QW_{Q'}$ is an Atkin--Lehner matrix $W_{QQ'}$ up to a scalar and an element of $\Gamma_0(L)$ \cite{AL}, so $W_{u_s}=\prod_{p\mid u}W_{p^{v_p(ds)}}$ on $M_2(\Gamma_0(L))$ and $\chi(u_s)=\prod_{p\mid u}\chi(p^{v_p(ds)})$; expanding the product gives the sum over $u$. For \eqref{eq:Rdsym}: in \eqref{eq:Rdecomp}, $c_{d_1}(d_1s_1)c_{d_2}(d_2s_2)=c_d(ds)$ with $s=s_1s_2$, and for fixed $s$ the pair $(s_1,s_2)=((s,d_1),(s,d_2))$ is determined by $(d_1,d_2)$, so the sum over $d_1d_2=d$ sums, for each $(t,t_1,s)$, the coefficients of $G_{t_1(d_1)_s,(d_2)_s}$ over all $d_1\mid d$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:cancel}
Let $d$ be odd and square-free, $s=1$, $t_1=1$, and let $f$ be a newform of level $2^eM_1\mid4d$ with $S(f)$, $\mathrm{pr}_f$ as in Proposition~\ref{prop:factor}. If $p\mid M_1$, $W_p$ acts on $S(f)$ as $\eta_p(f)$; if $p\mid d$, $p\nmid M_1$, $W_p$ permutes the basis $f(\delta\,\cdot)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:ALold}) and $S(f)$ splits into two eigenspaces of equal dimension; and $W_d$ acts on $G_{1,d}$ as $\chi(d)$ (Remark~\ref{rem:RSsym}(i)). Hence $P^+_{d,1}\mathrm{pr}_fG_{1,d}=0$ whenever $\eta_p(f)=-1$ for some $p\mid M_1$. Since $\mathrm{pr}_fG_{1,d}\ne0$ requires $L(1,f)L(1,f\otimes\chi)\ne0$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:RS}(ii)), hence the root number $\varepsilon(f)=-\eta_{2^e}(f)\eta_{M_1}(f)=+1$, and $\eta_4=-1$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:eta4}): for $e=0$, $M_1>1$, $\eta_{M_1}(f)=-1$ and the whole component cancels in $R_d$, i.e.\ \emph{every oldform of odd level cancels}; for $e=1$ the component survives only if $a_f(2)=+1$ and $\eta_p(f)=+1$ for all $p\mid M_1$; for $e=2$ (in particular for the newforms of level $4d$) it survives iff $\eta_p(f)=+1$ for all $p\mid M_1$. What survives enters $R_d$ with the factor $2^{\omega(d)}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The actions of $W_p$ are Lemma~\ref{lem:ALold} with $Q=p$; $P^+_{d,1}$ contains the factor $\frac12(1+W_p)$, which annihilates $S(f)$ when $W_p=-1$ there; the sign conditions are the functional equation in weight $2$, $\varepsilon(f)=-\eta_{\mathrm{level}}(f)$.
\end{proof}

For $d=p$ nothing cancels: $W_p=\chi(p)$ on $G_{1,p}$, and indeed $R(p,1)=R(1,p)$ by the symmetry $n\leftrightarrow N-n$. The cancellation is between the $2^{\omega(d)-1}$ unordered splittings; for $d=p_1p_2$ it is between $R(d,1)$ and $R(p_1,p_2)$, i.e.\ between $G_{1,d}$ and $G_{p_1,p_2}=G_{1,d}|_2W_{p_1}$, and it removes the $(-,-)$-eigenspace, which contains all the oldforms of odd level and half of the newforms of level $4d$. The script \texttt{symmetric\_remainder.py} computes every $R(d_1,d_2;N)$ exactly for $24$ values of $N\equiv2\pmod4$ near $2\cdot10^6$ and all admissible pairs with $d_1d_2\le N^{3/4}$ (\texttt{results/symmetric\_remainder.txt}): averaged over $N$, $\sum_{d\le N^\theta}|R_d|$ is $0.67$--$0.69$ of $\sum_{d_1d_2\le N^\theta}|R(d_1,d_2;N)|$ on the moduli with $\omega(d)=2$ and $0.46$--$0.48$ on those with $\omega(d)=3$, for $\theta=0.5,\ldots,0.75$, against $1/\sqrt2=0.707$ and $1/2$ for independent random signs on the unordered splittings; and $0.95$--$0.98$ of the random-sign value. The cancellation is thus exact in the sense of Proposition~\ref{prop:cancel} and of square-root strength, and no more, because the norm of $G_{1,d}$ is close to equidistributed over the joint eigenspaces: \texttt{symmetric\_norm\_share.py} (\texttt{results/symmetric\_norm\_share.txt}) finds, from the exact coefficients of all $G_{u,d/u}$ up to $2^{18}$, the Rankin--Selberg share $0.50$--$0.62$ for the $(+,+)$-eigenspace of $G_{1,p_1p_2}$ with both primes $\equiv1\pmod4$, but restricted to the coefficients $a(2n')-a(n')$, $n'\equiv1\pmod4$, that the sieve sees (at even $N$ the newforms of level $4d$ contribute nothing, as $a_\varphi(2)=0$), the share $0.39$--$0.47$, which matches the empirical value $0.424$ of the remainders themselves; for $\omega(d)=3$ and $4$ the sieve shares of the all-$(+)$ pattern are $0.20$ and $0.105$--$0.109$ against $1/4$ and $1/8$ for equidistribution. Since $2^{\omega(d)}=d^{o(1)}$, this gain is absorbed by the factor $N^\eta$ of condition (F1) in the proof of Part~I, Theorem~3.2, and does not change the level of distribution; what \eqref{eq:Rdsym} says is that the two-dimensional sieve for $n(N-n)$ sees, at each modulus $d$, only the $W_p$-invariant part of $G_{t_1,ds}$, and therefore none of the oldforms of odd level.

\subsection{The dimension, and mollified weights}\label{subsec:dimension}
The factor $2^{\Omega(n(N-n))}$ of the weight enters the count only through the division by $\max w$ in \eqref{eq:count2}, i.e.\ in the constant of Theorem~\ref{thm:dim2} and nowhere else: the pairs $(k,k')$ are decided by the thresholds $1/\lambda+2/b$, $1/\lambda+1/b$, which depend on the level $\theta$ and on the dimension $\kappa$. What the weights $r(n)r(N-n)$ really cost is the dimension. Under $w$ the density of $p\mid n(N-n)$ is $2h(p)\sim4/p$ for split $p$, dimension $2$; under the indicator weight $b(n)b(N-n)$ ($n$ and $N-n$ sums of two squares) it would be $\sim2/p$ for split $p$ and $\sim2/q^2$ for inert $q$ (as $pn'$ is a sum of two squares iff $n'$ is), dimension $1$, the dimension of Goldbach's problem; the factor $2$ in $r(pn')=2r(n')$ is this doubling of the density, not a weight that could be removed. Extracting a split prime $p\mid n$ does not help either: $n=pn'$ gives $w(n)=2r(n')r(N-pn')$, the sequence $\{n'\}$ with the weights $r(n')r(N-pn')$ of the pair $(p,1)$, again of dimension $2$; and Chen's switching trick would need the remainders $\sum_{p\sim P}(T(dp,e;N)-M(dp,e;N))$ for $de\le N^\theta$, $P\ge N^b$, i.e.\ cancellation over the prime variable in the sum over the moduli, which is the problem of Section~\ref{sec:beyond}.

Weights of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moll}
\tilde w(n)=r(n)\Bigl(\sum_{\substack{u\mid n\\ u\le N^\xi}}\gamma_u\Bigr)^2r(N-n)\Bigl(\sum_{\substack{v\mid N-n\\ v\le N^\xi}}\gamma_v\Bigr)^2
\end{equation}
(a Selberg-type mollifier) stay inside the theta framework, since $\sum_{d_1\mid n,\,d_2\mid N-n}\tilde w(n)$ is a combination of the sums $S([d_1,u_1,u_2],[d_2,v_1,v_2])$ with moduli $\le d_1d_2N^{4\xi}$, controlled by Part~I, Theorem~3.2 at the level $1/2-4\xi$. They cannot lower the dimension where it matters.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:nothing}
Let $(\gamma_u)$ be any weights supported on $u\le N^\xi$ with $\gamma_1=1$, and $\xi<a$, $z=N^a$. Then $\tilde w(n)=w(n)$ for every $n$ with $(n(N-n),P(z))=1$; hence $S(\tilde{\cA},z)=S(\cA,z)$, $S(\tilde{\cA}_p,z)=S(\cA_p,z)$ for $p\ge z$, and the weighted sum $W$ of \eqref{eq:W2} is the same for $\tilde w$ and $w$: the sieve applied to $\tilde w$ bounds exactly what the sieve applied to $w$ bounds, for which the level $1/2$ is available, whereas the congruence sums of $\tilde w$ are controlled only at the level $1/2-4\xi$; nothing is gained by expanding the mollifier.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $(n,P(z))=1$, the only divisor $u\le N^\xi<z$ of $n$ is $u=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:density}
For $\gamma$ multiplicative, supported on square-free integers, and without truncation, the sequence $r(n)(\sum_{u\mid n}\gamma_u)^2$ has at a prime $p$ the density $\tilde h(p)=h(p)(1+\gamma_p)^2/(1+(2\gamma_p+\gamma_p^2)h(p))$: it equals $h(p)$ if $\gamma_p=0$, and $(1+\gamma_p)^2=\frac12$ gives $\tilde h(p)\sim1/p$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The main term of $\sum_{d\mid n}r(n)(\sum_{u\mid n}\gamma_u)^2$ is $X\sum_{u_1,u_2}\gamma_{u_1}\gamma_{u_2}h([d,u_1,u_2])$; for $d=1$ it is $X\prod_\ell(1+(2\gamma_\ell+\gamma_\ell^2)h(\ell))$ and for $d=p$ the Euler factor at $p$ becomes $h(p)(1+\gamma_p)^2$.
\end{proof}

So a mollifier changes the density exactly at the primes it contains: to act on the Richert range $[N^a,N^b]$ it must have length $\ge N^b$ and costs $4b$ (or $2b$ for a one-sided mollifier) in the level, more than the level itself; the choice $(1+\gamma_p)^2=\frac12$ for all $p$ gives $(\sum_{u\mid n}\gamma_u)^2=2^{-\omega(n)}$ and $\tilde w=16\,b(n)b(N-n)$ on the integers counted, the indicator weight above, so that the truncation at $N^\xi$ is exactly the trade between dimension and level. The script \texttt{mollified\_benchmark.py} (\texttt{results/mollified\_benchmark.txt}) scans the hybrid sieve for $\xi\ge a$ (dimension $1$, resp.\ $3/2$, below $N^\xi$, density $2$ above, level $1/2-4\xi$, resp.\ $1/2-2\xi$) and finds the best thresholds $8.574/5.603$ (two-sided) and $8.042/5.334$ (one-sided), both at $\xi=a$ and both worse than $7.561/5.065$.

\section{Beyond the level $1/2$: the sum over the moduli}\label{sec:beyond}

We end with the question that the sieve route leaves open for every $N$: the level of distribution beyond $1/2$. At the level $\theta$, the remainder of the sieve (the vector sieve of Part~I as well as the two-dimensional sieve of Section~\ref{sec:dim2}) is a sum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:remsum}
\sum_{\substack{de\le N^\theta\\ (d,e)=1,\ (de,2N)=1}}\lambda_d\lambda'_e\,R(d,e;N)
\end{equation}
with sieve weights $|\lambda_d|,|\lambda'_e|\le1$ supported on square-free integers, and by \eqref{eq:Rdecomp} each $R(d,e;N)$ is a bounded combination of coefficients $a_{G_{d',e'}}(N')$ with $d'e'\asymp de$ (up to divisors of $N$) and $N'\in\{N,N/2\}$. Part~I bounds \eqref{eq:remsum} by $\sum|R(d,e;N)|$ and each term by $N^{1/2+\e}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:norm}(ii)), which gives $N^{1/2+\e}N^{\theta}$ and hence $\theta<1/2$; Proposition~\ref{prop:beyond} shows that any power saving $(de)^{-\eta}$ in the individual bound raises the level. The other possibility is cancellation between the terms: $R(d,e;N)$ changes sign with $(d,e)$, and the sieve weights are sign-changing too.

\subsection{The structure of the sum}
Since $\sum_{d\mid n,\ e\mid N-n}r(n)r(N-n)=T(d,e;N)$ for coprime $d,e$, the sum \eqref{eq:remsum} is, before the M\"obius inversion of $(n,N)=1$,
\[
\sum_{d,e}\lambda_d\lambda'_e\bigl(T(d,e;N)-M(d,e;N)\bigr)=\sum_{d,e}\lambda_d\lambda'_e\Bigl(\sum_{d\alpha+e\beta=N}r(\alpha)r(\beta)-M(d,e;N)\Bigr),
\]
a weighted count of the representations of $N$ by the family of quaternary forms $Q_{d,e}$. Writing $n=d\alpha$ and $N-n=e\beta$, the main sum is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bilinear}
\sum_{e}\lambda'_e\sum_{\substack{d,\alpha\\ d\alpha\equiv N\ (e)}}\lambda_d\,r(\alpha)\,r\Bigl(\frac{N-d\alpha}e\Bigr),
\end{equation}
the distribution of the products $d\alpha$, weighted by $\lambda_dr(\alpha)$, in the residue class $N$ modulo $e$, with the additional weight $r((N-d\alpha)/e)$ attached to the quotient. Without the last factor this is the setting of the dispersion method of Bombieri, Friedlander and Iwaniec \cite{BFI}: sums $\sum_q\lambda_q\sum_{mn\equiv a\ (q)}\alpha_m\beta_n$ with well-factorable $\lambda$, for which the level $4/7$ is known for suitable $\alpha,\beta$ from the Deshouillers--Iwaniec bounds for sums of Kloosterman sums. The factor $r((N-d\alpha)/e)=4\sum_{k\mid(N-d\alpha)/e}\chi(k)$ introduces a third modulus: \eqref{eq:bilinear} becomes $4\sum_{e,k}\lambda'_e\chi(k)\sum_{d\alpha\equiv N\ (ek)}\lambda_dr(\alpha)$, the distribution of $d\alpha$ in progressions to the moduli $ek$, where $k$ runs up to $N/(de)$, so that the moduli $ek$ range up to $N$. This is the shape of the Titchmarsh divisor problem, $\sum_{q}\sum_{mn\equiv a\ (q)}$ over all $q\le x$, which the dispersion method does handle (Fouvry \cite{Fouvry}, Bombieri--Friedlander--Iwaniec \cite{BFI}), with the difference that here both $d\alpha$ and the quotient carry the arithmetic weight $r$ and the modulus $e$ carries a sieve weight. We expect that the dispersion method, applied to \eqref{eq:bilinear} with the moduli $e$ in a range $N^{\theta_e}$ and the bilinear structure of $\lambda_dr(\alpha)$, gives a level $\theta>1/2$ for the signed sum \eqref{eq:remsum} and with it an improvement of Theorem~\ref{thm:dim2} for every $N$; we have not carried this out. The spectral version of the same question is the sum over the levels $4de$ of the inner products $\langle G_{d,e},P_N^{(4de)}\rangle$ of Section~\ref{sec:spectral}.

The Kloosterman structure of \eqref{eq:bilinear} can be made exact. The arithmetic input is the Fourier transform of the circle modulo $c$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:circle}
Let $c$ be odd, $(a,c)=1$ and $h=(h_1,h_2)\in\Z^2$, and for odd $c'$ let $c'^{*}$ be the product of the primes dividing $c'$ to an odd power ($c'^{*}=c'$ for square-free $c'$). Then
\[
F_c(a;h):=\sum_{\substack{x,y\bmod c\\ x^2+y^2\equiv a\ (c)}}e\Bigl(\frac{h_1x+h_2y}{c}\Bigr)=\sum_{g\mid(c,h_1,h_2)}g\,\chi\Bigl(\Bigl(\frac cg\Bigr)^{*}\Bigr)S\Bigl(a,\ \overline4\,\frac{h_1^2+h_2^2}{g^2};\ \frac cg\Bigr),
\]
with $S(m,n;c)=\sum^*_{t\bmod c}e((mt+n\bar t)/c)$ the classical Kloosterman sum; for prime $c$, $F_c(a;h)=\chi(c)S(a,\overline4|h|^2;c)+c[h\equiv0\ (c)]$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Detect the congruence by $\frac1c\sum_{t\bmod c}e(t(x^2+y^2-a)/c)$; for $(t,c)=g$ the sum over $x$ vanishes unless $g\mid h_1$ and then equals $g\,e(-\overline{4t'}(h_1/g)^2/(c/g))G(t';c/g)$ by completing the square, with $t=gt'$ and $G(t';c')=\sum_{x\bmod c'}e(t'x^2/c')$; for a prime power $G(t';p^\nu)^2=p^\nu$ if $\nu$ is even and $\chi(p)p^\nu$ if $\nu$ is odd, so that $G(t';c')^2=\chi(c'^{*})c'$ for every odd $c'$. Collecting the terms with the same $g$ gives the formula (\texttt{kloosterman\_check.py} verifies it for all $a,h$ and all odd primes $c\le61$, for nine square-free composite $c\le105$ and for eleven odd $c\le135$ with square factors).
\end{proof}

So the Fourier transform of the circle is a classical Kloosterman sum whose second argument is the norm of the dual variable, twisted only by the sign $\chi(c)$; this is why $r(n)$ in arithmetic progressions behaves like the divisor function (the Weil bound gives the asymptotic for $c\le Y^{2/3-\e}$). Now let $m\equiv m'\equiv1\pmod4$ be coprime, $(mm',N)=1$, and $T^{\mathrm{odd}}(m,m';N)$ the part of $T(m,m';N)$ with $\alpha$ odd, so that $a_G(N)-a_G(N/2)=T^{\mathrm{odd}}-M^{\mathrm{odd}}$ in \eqref{eq:Rdecomp}. Every term of $T^{\mathrm{odd}}$ with $r(\alpha)r(\beta)\ne0$ has $\alpha\equiv\beta\equiv1\pmod 4$, so $r(\beta)=8\sum_{k\mid\beta,\,k<\sqrt\beta}\chi(k)+4\chi(\sqrt\beta)[\beta=\square]$, and with $\beta=(N-m\alpha)/m'$ (note $(k,m)=1$ automatically)
\begin{gather*}
T^{\mathrm{odd}}(m,m';N)=8\sum_{k<\sqrt{N/m'}}\chi(k)\,\mathcal N_k+O(N^{1/2+\e}m'^{-1/2}),\\
\mathcal N_k=\#\bigl\{(x,y):\ x+y\text{ odd},\ x^2+y^2\equiv N\bar m\ (m'k),\ 0<x^2+y^2<Y_k\bigr\},
\end{gather*}
$Y_k=(N-m'k^2)/m$, the error being the ternary term $m(x^2+y^2)+m'j^2=N$. Poisson summation on the classes modulo $2c$, $c=m'k$ (with a smooth cutoff at $Y_k$), gives $\mathcal N_k=\frac{Y_k}{2c^2}\sum_{h\in\Z^2,\,h_1\equiv h_2\,(2)}\hat w(h\sqrt{Y_k}/(2c))F_c(N\bar m;\bar2h)$; the term $h=0$ summed over $k$ is the Siegel main term $M^{\mathrm{odd}}(m,m';N)$, and in the leading term $g=1$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:circle} (the modulus $c=m'k$ need not be square-free, as $k$ is any divisor of $\beta$) the character of the divisor expansion is cancelled by the sign of the Gauss sums, $\chi(k)\chi((m'k)^{*})=\chi(k)\chi(k^{*})=\chi(k/k^{*})=1$ because $k/k^{*}$ is an odd square, while $S(N\bar m,\overline{16}|h|^2;c)=S(16N,m|h|^2;c)$. Hence:

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:dispersion}
With the notation above,
\begin{multline}\label{eq:dispmain}
a_G(N)-a_G(N/2)=8\sum_{k<\sqrt{N/m'}}\frac{Y_k}{2(m'k)^2}\sum_{\substack{h\ne0\\ h_1\equiv h_2\ (2)}}\hat w\Bigl(\frac{h\sqrt{Y_k}}{2m'k}\Bigr)S\bigl(16N,\ m|h|^2;\ m'k\bigr)\\
+(\text{terms with }g>1)+O(N^{1/2+\e}m'^{-1/2})+\Sigma_\partial ,
\end{multline}
$\Sigma_\partial$ the smoothing error, and the remainder sum \eqref{eq:remsum}, after the decomposition \eqref{eq:Rdecomp} and with $m\le m'$ by the symmetry $n\leftrightarrow N-n$, has as its leading term (the contribution of the terms $g=1$; those with $g>1$ are sums of the same shape with the modulus $m'k/g$ and the dual variable $h/g$) the trilinear form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trilinear}
\sum_{m'}\Lambda'_{m'}\sum_{c\equiv0\ (m')}\ \sum_{m}\Lambda_m\sum_{j}b_{m,j}(c)\,S(16N,\ mj;\ c),\qquad |\Lambda_m|,|\Lambda'_{m'}|\le3^{\omega},
\end{equation}
with the modulus $c=m'k\equiv0\pmod{m'}$, $\sqrt{N/m}\ll c\le\sqrt{Nm'}$, the first argument $16N$ fixed, the second argument $n=mj\ll m^2m'$ with $j$ a norm, and smooth weights in $c$ and in $h$, $|h|\ll m'k\sqrt{m/N}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:dispersion}
(i) \emph{Bookkeeping.} In \eqref{eq:dispmain} each $k$ contributes $O(1)$ terms of weight $O(1)$; the ternary term is $O(N^{1/2+\e}m'^{-1/2})$ and its sum over $mm'\le N^\theta$ is harmless for $\theta<2/3$, the smoothing error is the usual price of a dyadic smoothing, and the terms with $g>1$ have fewer terms and smaller moduli; we do not carry these out and discuss the leading term. The Weil bound gives $|a_G(N)-a_G(N/2)|\ll N^\e\sum_{k\le\sqrt{N/m'}}(m'k)^{1/2}\ll N^{3/4+\e}m'^{-1/4}$ and the level $1/4$; Theorem~\ref{thm:norm}(ii), $N^{1/2+\e}$, is smaller by $(N/m')^{1/4}$, exactly the square root of the length of the $k$-sum: the uniform bound of Part~I is equivalent to square-root cancellation in the sum over the moduli $c\equiv0\pmod{m'}$ for this sequence of second arguments, and gives the level $1/2$. Square-root cancellation in the sum over $m'$ as well would give $N^{1/2+\e}\Delta E^{1/2}$ for $m\le\Delta$, $m'\le E$, hence the level $2/3$ for the balanced split $\Delta=E$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:beyond} with the saving $E^{-1/2}$), and cancellation over $m$ too the level $1-\e$; the data of Table~\ref{tab:signs} are consistent with both.

(ii) \emph{The fixed first argument.} The sum over the modulus is of the type treated by Deshouillers and Iwaniec \cite{DI} and by Drappeau \cite{Drappeau}, but with a single first argument $16N\asymp N\gg c$; the spectral large sieve then costs the term $16N/m'$ on that side, which makes the Kuznetsov route no better than the Weil bound, and the general bilinear bounds do not apply because the Kloosterman sum is already complete and the congruence is quadratic. Nor can the first argument be dualised away through $h$: the box of $h$ has side $H=m'k\sqrt{m/N}\le\sqrt{mm'}$, the square root of the sieve level, while the modulus of a Poisson step in $h$ for the off-diagonal terms of a Cauchy--Schwarz over $h$ is $[c_1,c_2]\ge\sqrt{N/m}$, so that the dual sum is longer than the sum itself by at least $N^{1/2-\theta/4}$ and the exchange is a loss. A Cauchy--Schwarz with the smooth variables $(k,h)$ outside and $m'$ inside loses already in its diagonal (which is the Weil mean square of Kloosterman sums over $h$) and proves at most the level $2/5$. These computations are carried out in the working notes kept in the directory \texttt{notes/} of the repository; they identify the obstacle, not a way around it. The two-sided problem is the analogue of Linnik's $N=p+x^2+y^2$ \cite{Linnik}, with both sides carrying $r$ and sieve weights.
\end{remark}

\begin{gather*}
A=\sum|a_{d,e}|,\qquad Q=\Bigl(\sum a_{d,e}^2\Bigr)^{1/2},\\
S_1=\sum a_{d,e},\qquad S_\mu=\sum\mu(de)\,a_{d,e},\qquad S_{\mathrm{rnd}}=\sum\pm a_{d,e}
